范围、对象与信息丢失问题

本章围绕映射及其约束展开

本单元覆盖2016年官方在线版本第2章 Linear Algebra，印刷页码29至50页。已读取2.1至2.12的全部正文。范围不等于整本书，第1章已有单独笔记，后续章节及本章引用的外部文献不在本次覆盖内。

本笔记的问题主线是线性映射保留、消除或放大了什么。形状决定运算是否合法，秩决定哪些信息能够恢复，正交性控制几何关系，谱分解揭示各个方向的缩放。PCA最终在明确的重构目标下选择需要保留的方向。

定义与公式编号注明原章依据。下文的证明、反例、数值表和成本核算均为笔记独立推导，原图另行标识。它们不是实验测量，也不是全章翻译。本章的PCA是数学推导示例，并没有实证基准或消融实验。

乘法表示复合，广播则是一种约定

匹配的维度具有数学意义

2.1与2.2节第32至34页区分普通矩阵乘法、逐元素乘法和广播。如果B先将输入映射到中间空间，A再将中间空间映射到输出，那么AB先执行B。求和索引连接这两个空间。Hadamard积只乘对应坐标，不包含这一步中间求和。

A ∈ ℝᵐˣⁿ, B ∈ ℝⁿˣᵖ ⇒ AB ∈ ℝᵐˣᵖ; (AB)ᵢⱼ=Σₖ₌₁ⁿ AᵢₖBₖⱼ. (A⊙B)ᵢⱼ=AᵢⱼBᵢⱼ for equal shapes.

独立例子取A=[[1,2],[0,1]]、B=[[2,0],[1,3]]。下表的三种运算均有定义，但结果不同。结合律仍允许将A(BC)改写成(AB)C，并不允许交换因子顺序。转置会反转因子顺序，这可以由逐元素求和中两个自由索引的交换推出。

(AB)ᵀ=BᵀAᵀ; xᵀy=yᵀx for real vectors. A(B+C)=AB+AC; A(BC)=(AB)C.

偏置项需要轴约定

样本按行存储时，原章以C_ij=A_ij+b_j定义A+b，特征偏置在各行重复。显式写法为A+1_m bᵀ，其中b有n个条目。其他数组库可能采用不同的轴对齐和扩展规则，简写本身不能确定某个库的广播行为。

C=A+1ₘbᵀ; Cᵢⱼ=Aᵢⱼ+bⱼ, A ∈ ℝᵐˣⁿ, b ∈ ℝⁿ. Dense AB: mnp multiplications and mp(n−1) additions for n≥1.

秩区分可达性与唯一性

逆矩阵意味着双向恢复

2.3与2.4节第34至37页将线性方程组写成Ax=b。每一行提供一个约束，每一列给出一个由x中对应系数控制的方向。b属于列空间时恰好可达。若A存在非零零空间向量，不同系数便可能产生相同目标。

Ax=Σⱼ xⱼA_:,ⱼ. Ax=b is consistent ⇔ b∈col(A). If Ax₀=b, all solutions are x=x₀+z with z∈null(A).

最后一句可以双向证明。加入零空间向量不会改变Ax，而任意两个解相减必在零空间中。因此实数线性方程组不可能恰有两个或三个不同的解：只要存在非零零空间方向，就存在整条仿射直线上的无穷多个解。

对原章张成空间论证的秩形式整理。仅有形状不等式并不充分。

A=[[1,1],[2,2]]. col(A)=span{(1,2)ᵀ}; null(A)=span{(1,−1)ᵀ}.

在这个独立例子中，b=(3,6)ᵀ的解为x=(t,3−t)ᵀ，其中t可以是任意实数；目标(3,7)ᵀ则无解。两列两行并不能保证可逆。单位矩阵I_n保留所有向量，普通方阵逆满足A⁻¹A=AA⁻¹=I_n。

原章提醒，显式构造逆矩阵通常不是数值求解的合适途径。独立补充是，利用分解求解可以避免构造不必要的稠密逆矩阵，但无法消除问题本身的病态性。逆公式说明解是什么，并不保证有限精度下的准确度。

范数定义误差几何

大小、夹角与有意选择的惩罚

2.5节第37至38页将范数定义为满足正定性、绝对齐次性和三角不等式的大小函数。将损失解释为距离时，这些条件很重要。平方欧氏长度很有用，但它本身不是范数，非零条目计数也不满足齐次性。

‖x‖ₚ=(Σᵢ|xᵢ|ᵖ)¹/ᵖ, p≥1; ‖x‖∞=maxᵢ|xᵢ|. ‖A‖F²=ΣᵢⱼAᵢⱼ². xᵀy=‖x‖₂‖y‖₂cosθ for nonzero x,y.

平方长度的梯度在原点附近趋于零，而绝对值在零点具有非平凡的次梯度区间。这一区别有助于解释稀疏解，但L1惩罚并不总能恢复希望得到的支持集，特征相关性、损失曲率和惩罚强度也会影响结果。

标量例子隔离稀疏机制

arg min_t [½(t−a)²+λ|t|]=sign(a)·max(|a|−λ,0), λ≥0. arg min_t [½(t−a)²+(λ/2)t²]=a/(1+λ).

这个独立计算分别使用正值、负值和零点的次梯度条件。当a=0.3、λ=0.5时，第一个极小解为0，第二个为0.2。它解释了这一可分离二次问题中的阈值机制，并不证明任意神经网络训练都会达到稀疏全局最优。

结构矩阵保留不同性质

对角、对称与正交是不同性质

2.6节第38至40页介绍对角矩阵、对称矩阵、单位向量和正交性。对角结构限制坐标之间的混合；对称性要求矩阵等于自身转置；正交矩阵要求为方阵，行列向量都正交归一，只有垂直而长度任意的向量并不充分。

diag(v)x=v⊙x. diag(v)⁻¹=diag(1/v₁,…,1/vₙ) when every vᵢ≠0. QᵀQ=QQᵀ=I ⇒ Q⁻¹=Qᵀ.

对角乘法只需n次标量乘法，而直接稠密n乘n映射需要n²次。这是算术次数，不是实测加速比。矩形对角映射可以补零或丢弃坐标，不能具有普通的双侧逆。更低成本的表示也排除了许多可能的相互作用。

‖Qx‖₂²=xᵀQᵀQx=‖x‖₂². (Qx)ᵀ(Qy)=xᵀy. Q=[[0,−1],[1,0]] preserves lengths and rotates by 90°.

正交映射保留长度、夹角和两点间距离，但仍可显著改变坐标。对称矩阵未必保留这些量，例如diag(2,1)是对称矩阵，却会伸长一个轴。对角矩阵也可以含零条目并丢失信息。

子空间的正交归一基通常为矩形矩阵

D ∈ ℝᵈˣᵏ, DᵀD=Iₖ, k<d. P=DDᵀ ⇒ Pᵀ=P, P²=P, rank(P)=k; generally P≠I_d.

D的列使编码c的长度得到保留，因为‖Dc‖₂=‖c‖₂。其转置压缩任意x，并消除垂直于col(D)的分量。重构矩阵P是投影，不是对整个ℝᵈ上压缩操作的逆。这一区别是PCA的核心约束。

特征向量的解释依赖明确条件

不变方向与对称情形

2.7节第40至42页用Av=λv定义非零特征向量。如果方阵具有完备的线性无关特征向量组，就可写成A=VΛV⁻¹。这是实质条件。实矩阵可能需要复特征向量，亏损矩阵即使在复数域上也可能没有完备特征向量组。

A=Aᵀ ∈ ℝⁿˣⁿ ⇒ A=QΛQᵀ, QᵀQ=I, Λ=diag(λ₁,…,λₙ), λᵢ∈ℝ. Avᵢ=λᵢvᵢ.

二次型是特征值的加权平均

x=Qz, ‖x‖₂=1 ⇒ Σᵢzᵢ²=1 and xᵀAx=Σᵢλᵢzᵢ². minᵢλᵢ ≤ xᵀAx ≤ maxᵢλᵢ.

这个笔记推导解释了原章给出的Rayleigh极值：非负权重z_i²之和为1，在极端特征值对应的特征子空间中达到等号。对于实对称矩阵，正定意味着所有特征值为正，半正定允许零，分别等价于对所有非零x有xᵀAx>0，以及对所有x有xᵀAx≥0。

对称条件非常重要。非对称矩阵[[1,10],[0,1]]具有正特征值，但x=(1,−1)ᵀ时xᵀAx=−8。因此任意矩阵仅有正特征值，并不能证明二次型具有正曲率。这个反例由笔记补充。

即使特征值互不相同，特征向量仍可改变符号；重特征值还允许在对应子空间内进行正交旋转。因此对特征值排序并不能使特征向量矩阵在字面上唯一。稳定的解释往往属于子空间或投影，而不是单个坐标的符号。

SVD区分输入方向与输出方向

适用于矩形映射的分解

2.8节第42至43页给出任意实矩阵的奇异值分解。在完整分解约定下，U与V是方形正交矩阵，Σ是具有非负奇异值的矩形对角矩阵。V给出输入坐标，U给出输出坐标，奇异值连接两者，输入和输出方向不必相同。

A ∈ ℝᵐˣⁿ; A=UΣVᵀ, U ∈ ℝᵐˣᵐ, V ∈ ℝⁿˣⁿ, Σ ∈ ℝᵐˣⁿ. Avᵢ=σᵢuᵢ for σᵢ>0. σ₁≥σ₂≥…≥σ_min(m,n)≥0.
AᵀA=V(ΣᵀΣ)Vᵀ; AAᵀ=U(ΣΣᵀ)Uᵀ. Nonzero eigenvalues of both products are σᵢ². rank(A)=#{i:σᵢ>0}.

第二组恒等式通过约去UᵀU或VᵀV直接得到。它也解释了AᵀA为什么半正定，因为xᵀAᵀAx=‖Ax‖₂²。输入空间中的零奇异方向恰好是A消除的信息，输出空间中的零方向则描述A永远无法到达的取值。

小型矩形映射使几何关系清楚可见

A=[[3,0,0],[0,1,0]]. U=I₂, V=I₃, Σ=A. AᵀA=diag(9,1,0); AAᵀ=diag(9,1).
独立的精确SVD例子。零输入方向没有与之配对的非零输出奇异值。
输入方向输出奇异缩放含义
(1,0,0)ᵀ(3,0)ᵀ3第一坐标伸长
(0,1,0)ᵀ(0,1)ᵀ1第二坐标保留
(0,0,1)ᵀ(0,0)ᵀ0第三坐标丢失

对称不定矩阵的特征值可以为负，而奇异值仍非负。对于非对称矩阵，特征值大小不能完整描述放大效应。最大奇异值等于单位x上‖Ax‖₂的最大值。特征向量回答不变方向问题，奇异向量回答伸缩问题。

伪逆选择最小范数最小二乘解

统一处理各种秩与形状

2.9节第43至44页利用SVD构造Moore Penrose伪逆：将非零奇异值取倒数，零保持为零。A⁺的形状为n乘m。当A是可逆方阵时，它等于普通逆，但不能恢复A已经消除的分量。

A=UΣVᵀ ⇒ A⁺=VΣ⁺Uᵀ; (Σ⁺)ᵢᵢ=1/σᵢ for σᵢ>0, otherwise 0. x*=A⁺b = arg min_(x∈arg min_z ‖Az−b‖₂) ‖x‖₂.

独立推导是在y=Vᵀx、β=Uᵀb坐标下，将平方残差写成非零奇异方向上的(σ_i y_i−β_i)²之和，再加上不可达输出项。确定的坐标为β_i/σ_i。零空间坐标无法降低残差，因此令其为零使范数最小。这个证明同时覆盖不一致和秩亏系统。

AA⁺b = projection of b onto col(A). All least squares minimizers: x=A⁺b+(I−A⁺A)w, w∈ℝⁿ. Aᵀ(Ax*−b)=0.

秩亏例子同时展示两类缺口

A=[[1,1],[0,0]], b=(2,1)ᵀ. A⁺=[[½,0],[½,0]], x*=(1,1)ᵀ, Ax*=(2,0)ᵀ.
独立的精确最小二乘计算。目标的第二坐标不在col(A)中，因此最小残差非零。
候选x‖Ax−b‖₂²‖x‖₂²解释
(1,1)ᵀ12最小残差且范数最小
(2,0)ᵀ14残差相同而范数较大
(3,−1)ᵀ110残差相同而范数较大
(0,0)ᵀ50更小范数不能补偿更差残差

原章还给出α趋于零时(AᵀA+αI)⁻¹Aᵀ的极限。当α为有限正数时，奇异方向的增益是σ/(σ²+α)，而不是1/σ，此时得到正则化估计，并非精确伪逆。未正则化求解中的小非零奇异值会放大噪声，数值秩阈值则会改变保留哪些方向。

For invertible A, κ₂(A)=σmax/σmin. A=diag(1,10⁻⁶): Δb=(0,10⁻⁶)ᵀ ⇒ Δx=(0,1)ᵀ.

这个条件数例子由笔记推导。右端项很小的扰动可以使解发生大得多的绝对变化。SVD揭示了机制，但分解本身不能修复缺失或测量不佳的信息。正则化引入了偏差与稳定性的选择，需要结合应用解释。

迹描述总能量，行列式描述体积

可以循环置换，不能任意换序

2.10与2.11节第44至45页介绍迹和行列式。迹是方阵对角条目之和。Tr(AB)=Tr(BA)可通过交换两个有限求和得到，即使AB与BA的方阵大小不同也成立。更多因子只可在相应乘积有定义时循环置换。

A∈ℝᵐˣⁿ, B∈ℝⁿˣᵐ: Tr(AB)=ΣᵢⱼAᵢⱼBⱼᵢ=Tr(BA). Tr(ABC)=Tr(BCA)=Tr(CAB). ‖A‖F²=Tr(AᵀA)=Tr(AAᵀ).

独立反例取A=[[1,0],[0,0]]、B=[[0,1],[0,0]]、C=[[0,0],[1,0]]，则Tr(ABC)=1而Tr(ACB)=0。迹不能使不交换的矩阵因子任意换序，PCA目标的化简需要遵守这一区别。

保体积弱于保距离

For square A: det(A)=Πᵢλᵢ; |det(A)|=Πᵢσᵢ. |det(A)| is the volume scaling factor. det(A)=0 ⇔ rank(A)<n.
独立算术区分有向体积、绝对体积与度量几何。
ℝ²中的映射det(A)奇异值几何作用
diag(2,½)12, ½保面积，但通常改变长度
diag(−1,1)−11, 1保面积与长度，反转方向
diag(1,0)01, 0面积坍缩到直线

迹和行列式概括映射的不同侧面。对称散布矩阵的迹将各方向能量相加，行列式则将方向缩放因子相乘，任何方向坍缩都会使其为零。两种统计量都不能单独确定每个方向及其对预测目标的重要性。

PCA先为固定解码器求编码

目标函数规定什么信息重要

2.12节第45至50页在平方欧氏误差下推导线性重构。本笔记使用N个样本、d个特征和k个编码坐标，其中k<d，对应原章的m、n、l。解码器D为d乘k且各列正交归一，并不是方形正交矩阵。

g(c)=Dc, D∈ℝᵈˣᵏ, DᵀD=Iₖ. c*(x)=arg min_c ‖x−Dc‖₂². X∈ℝᴺˣᵈ has x⁽ⁱ⁾ᵀ in row i.

从重构转为迹最大化

奇异值给出精确误差预算

对X作SVD X=UΣVᵀ，可从最大的右奇异向量得到同一D。保留一个分量会从残差中移除它的平方奇异值。这描述给定数据上的重构能量，并不能证明类别信息、因果性、稳健性或未见样本表现。若全部数据为零，任意合规D的残差都为零，而能量比例没有定义。

Scope, objects and the problem of lost information

A chapter about maps and their constraints

This unit covers Chapter 2, Linear Algebra, printed pages 29 to 50 of the official 2016 online edition. All sections from 2.1 through 2.12 were read. The whole book is not covered. Chapter 1 has a separate note. Later chapters and the external references cited by this chapter are outside this unit.

The organizing question of this note is what a linear map preserves, removes or amplifies. Shape determines which operations exist. Rank determines what can be recovered. Orthogonality controls geometry. Spectral decompositions expose direction dependent scaling. PCA then chooses which directions to retain under one explicit reconstruction objective.

Multiplication is composition, while broadcasting is a convention

Compatible dimensions carry mathematical meaning

Sections 2.1 and 2.2, pp.32 to 34, distinguish ordinary multiplication, elementwise multiplication and broadcasting. If B maps an input into an intermediate space and A maps that space into an output, AB applies B first. The summed index links the two spaces. A Hadamard product instead multiplies matching coordinates without that intermediate sum.

A ∈ ℝᵐˣⁿ, B ∈ ℝⁿˣᵖ ⇒ AB ∈ ℝᵐˣᵖ; (AB)ᵢⱼ=Σₖ₌₁ⁿ AᵢₖBₖⱼ. (A⊙B)ᵢⱼ=AᵢⱼBᵢⱼ for equal shapes.

Independent example: A=[[1,2],[0,1]] and B=[[2,0],[1,3]]. The products below differ even though all three operations are defined. Associativity still permits regrouping A(BC)=(AB)C. It does not permit reordering factors. Transposition reverses their order, which follows by exchanging the two free indices in the entrywise sum.

Rank separates reachability from uniqueness

An inverse is a two sided recovery claim

Sections 2.3 and 2.4, pp.34 to 37, express a linear system as Ax=b. Each row is a constraint. Each column is a direction whose coefficient appears in x. The target b is reachable exactly when it belongs to the column space. Distinct coefficients can produce the same target when A has a nonzero null vector.

Ax=Σⱼ xⱼA_:,ⱼ. Ax=b is consistent ⇔ b∈col(A). If Ax₀=b, all solutions are x=x₀+z with z∈null(A).

The final statement follows in both directions. Adding a null vector preserves Ax. Subtracting any two solutions yields a null vector. It therefore explains why a real linear system cannot have exactly two or three distinct solutions: a nonzero null direction gives a full affine line of solutions.

Exact recovery does not imply stable recovery

A=[[1,1],[2,2]]. col(A)=span{(1,2)ᵀ}; null(A)=span{(1,−1)ᵀ}.

In this independent example, b=(3,6)ᵀ has solutions x=(t,3−t)ᵀ for any real t. The target (3,7)ᵀ is inconsistent. Two columns and two rows do not guarantee invertibility. The identity matrix I_n preserves all vectors; an ordinary square inverse satisfies A⁻¹A=AA⁻¹=I_n.

The source cautions that explicitly forming an inverse is usually a poor numerical route to a solve. Independent clarification: solving with a factorization avoids constructing an unnecessary dense inverse, but does not remove an ill conditioned problem. An inverse formula states what the solution is. It does not supply an accuracy guarantee at finite precision.

Size, angle and a deliberate choice of penalty

Section 2.5, pp.37 to 38, treats norms as size functions with definiteness, absolute homogeneity and the triangle inequality. These conditions matter when a loss is interpreted as distance. Squared Euclidean length is useful but is not itself a norm. Counting nonzero entries also fails the homogeneity requirement.

Structured matrices preserve different things

Diagonal, symmetric and orthogonal are distinct properties

Section 2.6, pp.38 to 40, introduces diagonal and symmetric matrices, unit vectors and orthogonality. Diagonal structure restricts how coordinates mix. Symmetry equates a matrix with its transpose. Orthogonality requires a square matrix with orthonormal rows and columns, not merely perpendicular vectors of arbitrary lengths.

diag(v)x=v⊙x. diag(v)⁻¹=diag(1/v₁,…,1/vₙ) when every vᵢ≠0. QᵀQ=QQᵀ=I ⇒ Q⁻¹=Qᵀ.

A diagonal multiplication uses n scalar products rather than n² for a direct dense n by n map. This is an arithmetic count, not a measured speedup. A rectangular diagonal map can append zeros or discard coordinates. It has no ordinary two sided inverse. The less costly representation also excludes many possible interactions.

‖Qx‖₂²=xᵀQᵀQx=‖x‖₂². (Qx)ᵀ(Qy)=xᵀy. Q=[[0,−1],[1,0]] preserves lengths and rotates by 90°.

An orthogonal map preserves lengths, angles and pairwise distances. It can still change coordinates substantially. A symmetric matrix need not preserve any of those quantities: diag(2,1) is symmetric and stretches one axis. A diagonal matrix can have zero entries and lose information.

An orthonormal basis for a subspace is rectangular

D ∈ ℝᵈˣᵏ, DᵀD=Iₖ, k<d. P=DDᵀ ⇒ Pᵀ=P, P²=P, rank(P)=k; generally P≠I_d.

The columns of D preserve the length of a code c because ‖Dc‖₂=‖c‖₂. Its transpose compresses an arbitrary x and removes the component perpendicular to col(D). The reconstruction P is a projector, not an inverse of that compression on all of ℝᵈ. This distinction becomes the central constraint in PCA.

Eigenvectors explain a map only under stated conditions

Invariant directions and the symmetric case

Section 2.7, pp.40 to 42, defines a nonzero eigenvector by Av=λv. If a square matrix has a full independent eigenbasis, it admits A=VΛV⁻¹. This condition is substantive. A real matrix can need complex eigenvectors, and a defective matrix can lack a full eigenbasis even over the complex numbers.

A=Aᵀ ∈ ℝⁿˣⁿ ⇒ A=QΛQᵀ, QᵀQ=I, Λ=diag(λ₁,…,λₙ), λᵢ∈ℝ. Avᵢ=λᵢvᵢ.

A quadratic form is a weighted average of eigenvalues

x=Qz, ‖x‖₂=1 ⇒ Σᵢzᵢ²=1 and xᵀAx=Σᵢλᵢzᵢ². minᵢλᵢ ≤ xᵀAx ≤ maxᵢλᵢ.

This note derivation explains the Rayleigh extremum stated in the chapter. The nonnegative weights z_i² sum to one. Equality is reached in an extreme eigenspace. For a real symmetric matrix, positive definiteness means every eigenvalue is positive, while positive semidefiniteness allows zeros. These are equivalent to xᵀAx>0 for every nonzero x and xᵀAx≥0 for every x, respectively.

The symmetry qualification is essential. The nonsymmetric matrix [[1,10],[0,1]] has positive eigenvalues, but x=(1,−1)ᵀ gives xᵀAx=−8. Positive eigenvalues alone therefore do not establish positive quadratic curvature for an arbitrary matrix. This is a counterexample added by the note.

An eigenvector can change sign even when its eigenvalue is distinct. Repeated eigenvalues permit orthonormal rotations within their eigenspaces. Sorting eigenvalues therefore does not make the matrix of eigenvectors literally unique. Stable interpretation often belongs to a subspace or projector rather than the signs of individual coordinates.

SVD separates input directions from output directions

A decomposition that includes rectangular maps

Section 2.8, pp.42 to 43, gives the singular value decomposition for every real matrix. U and V are square orthogonal matrices in the full convention. Σ is a rectangular diagonal matrix with nonnegative singular values. V describes input coordinates, U describes output coordinates, and the singular values connect them. They need not be the same directions.

A ∈ ℝᵐˣⁿ; A=UΣVᵀ, U ∈ ℝᵐˣᵐ, V ∈ ℝⁿˣⁿ, Σ ∈ ℝᵐˣⁿ. Avᵢ=σᵢuᵢ for σᵢ>0. σ₁≥σ₂≥…≥σ_min(m,n)≥0.
AᵀA=V(ΣᵀΣ)Vᵀ; AAᵀ=U(ΣΣᵀ)Uᵀ. Nonzero eigenvalues of both products are σᵢ². rank(A)=#{i:σᵢ>0}.

The second pair of identities follows directly by cancelling UᵀU or VᵀV. It also explains why AᵀA is positive semidefinite: xᵀAᵀAx=‖Ax‖₂². Zero singular directions in input space are precisely the information that A removes. Zero output directions describe values that A can never reach.

A small rectangular map makes the geometry explicit

A=[[3,0,0],[0,1,0]]. U=I₂, V=I₃, Σ=A. AᵀA=diag(9,1,0); AAᵀ=diag(9,1).

The pseudoinverse chooses a minimum norm least squares solution

One statement covers every rank and shape

Section 2.9, pp.43 to 44, constructs the Moore Penrose pseudoinverse from the SVD. Each nonzero singular value is reciprocated and zeros remain zero. The resulting A⁺ has shape n by m. It agrees with the ordinary inverse when A is square and invertible. It does not restore a component that A erased.

A=UΣVᵀ ⇒ A⁺=VΣ⁺Uᵀ; (Σ⁺)ᵢᵢ=1/σᵢ for σᵢ>0, otherwise 0. x*=A⁺b = arg min_(x∈arg min_z ‖Az−b‖₂) ‖x‖₂.

Independent derivation: in coordinates y=Vᵀx and β=Uᵀb, the squared residual is a sum of (σ_i y_i−β_i)² over nonzero singular directions, plus the unreachable output terms. Each determined coordinate is β_i/σ_i. Coordinates in null(A) do not reduce the residual, so setting them to zero minimizes the norm. This proof covers inconsistent and rank deficient systems together.

AA⁺b = projection of b onto col(A). All least squares minimizers: x=A⁺b+(I−A⁺A)w, w∈ℝⁿ. Aᵀ(Ax*−b)=0.

A rank deficient example has both kinds of ambiguity

A=[[1,1],[0,0]], b=(2,1)ᵀ. A⁺=[[½,0],[½,0]], x*=(1,1)ᵀ, Ax*=(2,0)ᵀ.

Trace measures total energy, while determinant measures volume

Cyclic order can change, arbitrary order cannot

Sections 2.10 and 2.11, pp.44 to 45, introduce trace and determinant. Trace is the sum of diagonal entries of a square matrix. The identity Tr(AB)=Tr(BA) follows from exchanging two finite summations. It remains true when AB and BA have different square sizes. More factors can be cycled only when the resulting products are defined.

A∈ℝᵐˣⁿ, B∈ℝⁿˣᵐ: Tr(AB)=ΣᵢⱼAᵢⱼBⱼᵢ=Tr(BA). Tr(ABC)=Tr(BCA)=Tr(CAB). ‖A‖F²=Tr(AᵀA)=Tr(AAᵀ).

Independent counterexample: A=[[1,0],[0,0]], B=[[0,1],[0,0]] and C=[[0,0],[1,0]] give Tr(ABC)=1 but Tr(ACB)=0. A trace does not make noncommuting matrix factors freely interchangeable. This distinction is used in the PCA objective reduction.

For square A: det(A)=Πᵢλᵢ; |det(A)|=Πᵢσᵢ. |det(A)| is the volume scaling factor. det(A)=0 ⇔ rank(A)<n.

PCA first solves the code for a fixed decoder

The objective specifies what information counts

Section 2.12, pp.45 to 50, derives linear reconstruction under squared Euclidean error. This note uses N examples, d features and k code coordinates, with k<d. The source calls these dimensions m, n and l. The decoder D has shape d by k and orthonormal columns. It is not a square orthogonal matrix.

g(c)=Dc, D∈ℝᵈˣᵏ, DᵀD=Iₖ. c*(x)=arg min_c ‖x−Dc‖₂². X∈ℝᴺˣᵈ has x⁽ⁱ⁾ᵀ in row i.

The source’s equations 2.54 to 2.67 expand and differentiate this objective. An equivalent independent derivation completes a square. Dᵀx is the component inside the decoder subspace. The orthogonal residual cannot be changed by c, so there is one optimal code for a fixed D.

‖x−Dc‖₂² = ‖c−Dᵀx‖₂² + xᵀ(I−DDᵀ)x. Therefore c*=Dᵀx and r(x)=DDᵀx.
x−DDᵀx ⟂ col(D); ‖x‖₂²=‖Dᵀx‖₂²+‖x−DDᵀx‖₂². ∇c‖x−Dc‖₂²=2c−2Dᵀx; Hessian=2Iₖ.

The Hessian establishes a strict minimum in code space. It does not make the joint search over all decoder matrices unconstrained or convex. With a full column rank decoder without the orthonormal constraint, the code instead satisfies c*=(DᵀD)⁻¹Dᵀx. The convenient encoder Dᵀ therefore depends on the constraint, not just on linear decoding.

Global reconstruction is a subspace choice

C=XD; X̂=CDᵀ=XDDᵀ. J(D)=‖X−XDDᵀ‖F², subject to DᵀD=Iₖ.

Here C contains one code per row and X̂=CDᵀ is the decoded data. The shared decoder must fit all examples, so minimizing each code separately does not complete PCA. The remaining problem chooses the k dimensional subspace.

PCA then retains the largest spectral energies

From reconstruction to a trace maximization

The chapter proves the case k=1 through equations 2.68 to 2.84 and states the extension to multiple components. The following independent proof covers general k directly. With S=XᵀX and P=DDᵀ, symmetry and idempotence give ‖X−XP‖F²=Tr(S)−Tr(DᵀSD). The discarded constant measures total squared input magnitude.

J(D)=Tr((I−P)S(I−P))=Tr(S)−Tr(SP)=Tr(S)−Tr(DᵀSD), P²=P=Pᵀ.
S=Q diag(λ₁,…,λ_d)Qᵀ, λ₁≥…≥λ_d≥0; Z=QᵀD, ZᵀZ=Iₖ. Tr(DᵀSD)=Σⱼ λⱼwⱼ, wⱼ=Σₐ₌₁ᵏZⱼₐ².

The weights obey 0≤w_j≤1 and sum to k. The upper bound follows because ZZᵀ is an orthogonal projector and its diagonal entries lie between zero and one. Under these constraints the weighted sum cannot exceed the sum of the largest k eigenvalues. Placing the columns of D in their eigenspaces attains the bound. This argument identifies a global optimum without a claim about a particular iterative training algorithm.

D*=Q_:,1:k; min_D J(D)=Σⱼ₌ₖ₊₁ᵈ λⱼ=Σⱼ₌ₖ₊₁ᵈ σⱼ(X)². Retained energy fraction=(Σⱼ₌₁ᵏλⱼ)/(Σⱼ₌₁ᵈλⱼ), when Tr(S)>0. σⱼ(X)=0 for j>min(N,d).

For k=1 this reduces to the Rayleigh problem dᵀSd with ‖d‖₂=1. For general k, every orthonormal basis of the same optimal subspace has the same reconstruction. A rotation D→DR with RᵀR=I_k changes the code coordinates while preserving DDᵀ. If λ_k=λ_(k+1), even the choice of the retained boundary subspace can be nonunique.

The singular values provide an exact error budget

An SVD X=UΣVᵀ supplies the same D from the leading right singular vectors. Keeping a component removes its squared singular value from the residual. This is reconstruction energy on the given data. It does not establish class information, causality, robustness or performance on unseen samples. With all data zero, the residual is zero for every admissible D and the energy fraction is undefined.

Centering, a complete numerical example and practical limits

Origin based reconstruction and variance are different objectives

The source derivation uses X directly and does not introduce mean subtraction. Its decoder reconstructs through the origin. The common centered covariance interpretation needs an extra operation. With μ equal to the sample mean and X_c=X−1_Nμᵀ, an affine reconstruction is μ+DDᵀ(x−μ). Then the eigenvectors of X_cᵀX_c also diagonalize the sample covariance X_cᵀX_c/(N−1), for N>1.

XᵀX=X_cᵀX_c+Nμμᵀ. Cov_sample=X_cᵀX_c/(N−1). x̂_affine=μ+DDᵀ(x−μ).

Independent example: four observations are (3,10), (−3,10), (0,11) and (0,9). Their mean is (0,10). The centered observations are (3,0), (−3,0), (0,1) and (0,−1). Costs and checks have explicit boundaries

Direct dense encoding and decoding: O(dk) work per example. Stored decoder and codes: dk+Nk numbers, versus Nd data numbers, excluding a mean and metadata. Compression requires k(N+d)<Nd under this accounting.

The storage comparison counts the shared decoder as well as all codes. It excludes precision changes, means and metadata. A dense SVD that computes the nonzero singular triplets can have arithmetic cost O(min(Nd²,N²d)); partial or iterative methods have different costs and convergence conditions. Forming XᵀX also squares the nonzero singular value ratio, which can worsen numerical conditioning. These are analytical observations, not measured runtime or memory results in the book.

The mathematical checks are dimension consistency, DᵀD≈I, reconstruction residual, and agreement between retained energy plus residual and total energy. Application questions remain separate. Rescaling a feature changes the objective. An outlier can dominate squared error. A small variance direction can still contain the label. PCA therefore needs a stated centering and scaling policy, an error target and an evaluation beyond training reconstruction when used for prediction.
