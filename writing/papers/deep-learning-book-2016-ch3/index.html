范围与主线：不确定性如何成为可计算对象

本单元完整读取官方在线版本第3章 Probability and Information Theory，印刷页码51至77页、3.1至3.14节及式3.1至3.56。这里处理的是一个章节，不是整本书。后续章节与本章引用的外部文献未作为本次全文阅读来源。

本笔记沿一个问题展开：模型如何描述尚未确定的状态，并在获得信息后更新描述。概率分布规定可能状态的权重；边缘化消去暂时不关心的变量；条件化改变参考信息；信息论衡量这种描述中的不确定性和失配；图模型将复杂联合分布拆成局部因子。

依据第52至54页的三类来源重新组织。表内限定是本笔记的解释。
3.1节的不确定性来源例子与解释不推出什么
系统随机性生成机制本身采用随机过程每个未知现象都必须本质随机
观测不完整决定结果的变量没有全部观察到重复观察一定消除全部不确定性
建模不完整离散化或压缩丢弃已观察的信息概率模型只能用于随机物理系统

频率解释把概率与重复试验的比例联系起来，贝叶斯解释把概率用于给定信息下的信念。两者使用相同的归一化、加法和条件化规则，但单个应用中应说明概率针对什么对象、哪些信息和哪个模型。概率一致性本身不能保证输入假设正确。

3.2节第54页区分随机变量和取值。下文采用大写X表示随机变量、小写x表示具体值，以避免原章不同字体在网页中混淆。原章定义与编号会注明。新增推导、反例、数字表和计算方法均为阅读笔记的独立分析，不是原书实验，也不是整章翻译。

概率质量与密度需要不同的归一化

3.3节第54至56页首先规定支持集。离散状态可以是有限或可数无穷的标签，并不一定是整数。PMF给每个状态直接分配概率。有限均匀分布的1/k只有在恰有k个状态且k为正整数时才成立。

P(X=x)≥0; ΣₓP(X=x)=1. For k finite states: P(X=xᵢ)=1/k, Σᵢ₌₁ᵏ1/k=1.

连续变量的密度相对于一个体积单位定义。p(x)可以超过1，而一个集合的概率仍不超过1，因为概率由积分给出。具有普通密度的连续变量在单个点上的概率为0，但该点可以属于分布的支持集。概率为0和逻辑上不可能在连续模型中不是同一个概念。

p(x)≥0; ∫p(x)dx=1; P(X∈S)=∫ₛp(x)dx. X∼U(a,b), b>a: p(x)=1/(b−a) on [a,b], and 0 elsewhere.

量独立例子 X∼U(0,1/4)含义
p(1/8)4单位长度上的密度
P(X=1/8)0单点概率
P(0≤X≤1/8)1/2区间内的质量

分号p(x;θ)将参数θ与随机状态x分开。密度值还会随坐标单位改变，因此比较不同单位中的密度峰值没有直接的概率意义。这也预示了3.12节的Jacobian修正：坐标范围缩小，密度必须增大，才能保持同一份质量。

边缘化、条件化与链式分解

3.4节第56至57页的边缘化是对未保留变量求和或积分，不是把它设置为某个值。3.5节的离散事件条件化则把联合概率重新归一化到已知事件内部。这两个操作回答不同问题。

连续情形不能将单点事件的0除以0作为条件密度。存在联合密度时，在pₓ(x)>0的位置可定义p(y|x)=p(x,y)/pₓ(x)，并对y积分得到条件事件概率。条件分布在边缘分布零测集上的版本未必唯一。这不是说连续变量无法条件化。

3.7节第58页将独立性定义为联合分布的乘积分解。条件独立是在给定信息后满足这种分解。对连续密度，等式按适当的几乎处处意义理解；对离散条件，只考虑正概率的条件状态。边缘独立和条件独立都不能自动推出另一个。

方差、协方差与非线性依赖

Var(X)=E[(X−E[X])²]=E[X²]−E[X]². Cov(X,Y)=E[(X−E[X])(Y−E[Y])]=E[XY]−E[X]E[Y].

3.8节第59至60页的二阶量要求相应二阶矩有限。独立性在此条件下推出零协方差；反方向一般不成立。协方差还随单位缩放，所以绝对值大不等于在不同问题间有更强的无量纲关系。方差非零时，相关系数用标准差归一化协方差。

Var(aX+bY)=a²Var(X)+b²Var(Y)+2abCov(X,Y). Σ=E[(X−μ)(X−μ)ᵀ]; Σᵢⱼ=Cov(Xᵢ,Xⱼ); Σᵢᵢ=Var(Xᵢ).

矩阵形式给出vᵀΣv=Var(vᵀX)≥0，所以协方差矩阵一定对称半正定，但不一定正定。前面P(A=1,B=1)=0.2、P(A=1,B=0)=0.3、P(A=0,B=1)=0.1的联合表的方差分别为0.25与0.21，协方差为0.05，因而Σ=[[0.25,0.05],[0.05,0.21]]。这些是总体分布的精确矩，不是从样本估计出来的无偏协方差。

原章反例先采X∼U(−1,1)，再独立采公平符号S∈{−1,1}，令Y=SX。由E[S]=0得到Cov(X,Y)=0，但|Y|=|X|，所以仍然依赖。下面的四阶矩检验是本笔记对该反例的独立推导。

E[X²]=E[Y²]=1/3; E[X²Y²]=E[X⁴]=1/5 ≠ E[X²]E[Y²]=1/9. E[XY]=E[S]E[X²]=0.

联合高斯是一个重要的特殊情况：其中零交叉协方差能够推出对应子向量独立。这个额外结论来自高斯密度的结构，不适用于任意边缘上分别呈高斯的变量，也不适用于仅仅检查一个数据表的线性相关性。

二元与类别分布：支持集决定参数含义

3.9.1节第60页的Bernoulli变量具有数值状态0与1，参数φ=P(X=1)属于[0,1]。因为X²=X，其期望和方差可立即从定义算出。端点φ=0或1表示退化的确定性分布，幂形式在端点按对应概率的连续延拓或分段定义解释。

P(X=1)=φ; P(X=0)=1−φ; P(X=x)=φˣ(1−φ)¹⁻ˣ, x∈{0,1}. E[X]=φ; Var(X)=φ(1−φ).

3.9.2节第60至61页的multinoulli也称categorical，表示一次k类选择。原章用k−1个自由参数，剩余概率由归一化确定；等价写法是使用k维非负向量π且和为1。类别名称不是数值，给类别任意编号后取均值通常没有不依赖编号的解释。

πᵢ≥0; Σᵢ₌₁ᵏπᵢ=1; P(C=i)=πᵢ. For one-hot Z: E[Z]=π, Cov(Z)=diag(π)−ππᵀ.

单变量高斯：尺度与精度是不同参数

3.9.3节第61至62页的高斯分布以μ为均值，以σ>0为标准差，第二个记号参数是方差σ²。分母中的σ并不等于方差。若改用精度β，必须令β=1/σ²，而不是1/σ。这些约定决定归一化常数和指数的单位。

N(x;μ,σ²)=[1/(√(2π)σ)]exp[−(x−μ)²/(2σ²)], μ∈ℝ, σ>0. β=σ⁻²>0; N(x;μ,β⁻¹)=√(β/(2π)) exp[−β(x−μ)²/2].

标准化Z=(X−μ)/σ把位置和尺度变换分开。独立例子取μ=2、σ=3、x=5，标准化值为1，密度为exp(−1/2)/(3√(2π))≈0.080657。若误把方差9放进原式的标准差位置，会同时破坏尺度和归一化。

原章用中心极限定理与最大不确定性解释高斯的常见性。这里需要保留条件：经典独立同分布、有限非零方差的标准化和才有相应高斯极限，独立性本身并不足够。高斯在固定均值与方差的连续密度类中最大化微分熵，也不意味着任何数据都应强制用单峰对称形状描述。

3.9.5节第63至64页的δ不是一个处处取有限实值的普通函数。其意义由积分确定：对适当的测试函数f，集中在μ的点质量返回f(μ)。这提供了将确定状态纳入概率语言的方式，不能把δ(0)当作一个可直接代入损失函数的有限密度。

∫f(x)δ(x−μ)dx=f(μ). p̂=(1/m)Σᵢ₌₁ᵐδ(x−x⁽ⁱ⁾); Eₚ̂[f(X)]=(1/m)Σᵢ₌₁ᵐf(x⁽ⁱ⁾).

经验分布在每个样本索引上放1/m的质量；重复观测的质量相加。离散数据{a,a,b}给出P̂(a)=2/3、P̂(b)=1/3。连续样本的经验分布是原子测度，而不是相对于长度或体积的普通PDF。它精确描述重采样训练集的操作，不自动描述总体的光滑密度。

原章把经验分布与最大似然联系起来，这句话需按模型类理解。在有限离散支持上，不受约束的类别似然由经验频率最大化；在任意连续普通密度类中，可令样本附近的峰越来越窄而使似然无界。将Dirac测度直接称作普通连续密度的最大似然解会混淆这两种情况。本笔记不声称已阅读全文第5章。

This unit covers the complete official online Chapter 3, Probability and Information Theory, printed pages 51 to 77, sections 3.1 to 3.14 and equations 3.1 to 3.56. It is one chapter of the book. Later chapters and externally cited works are outside this full reading.

The organizing question is how a model represents unresolved states and changes that representation after receiving information. A distribution weights possible states. Marginalization removes variables from the question. Conditioning changes the available information. Information theory measures uncertainty and mismatch. Graphical models organize a joint distribution into local factors.

Probability mass and density use different normalization

Section 3.3 on pages 54 to 56 starts with the state space. Discrete states may be labels and may form a countably infinite set. A PMF assigns probability directly to each state. The finite uniform formula 1/k assumes exactly k states and a positive integer k.

P(X=x)≥0; ΣₓP(X=x)=1. For k finite states: P(X=xᵢ)=1/k, Σᵢ₌₁ᵏ1/k=1.

A density is defined relative to a volume unit. Its value may exceed one. A set probability remains at most one because it is an integral. A continuous variable with an ordinary density gives each individual point zero probability. Such a point can still lie in its support. Zero probability is therefore distinct from logical impossibility.

Variance, covariance and nonlinear dependence

Var(X)=E[(X−E[X])²]=E[X²]−E[X]². Cov(X,Y)=E[(X−E[X])(Y−E[Y])]=E[XY]−E[X]E[Y].

The second order quantities on pages 59 to 60 require finite second moments. Independence then implies zero covariance. The converse is generally false. Covariance also changes with units. A larger magnitude across different problems need not mean a stronger dimensionless relationship. Correlation divides by both standard deviations when these are nonzero.

Var(aX+bY)=a²Var(X)+b²Var(Y)+2abCov(X,Y). Σ=E[(X−μ)(X−μ)ᵀ]; Σᵢⱼ=Cov(Xᵢ,Xⱼ); Σᵢᵢ=Var(Xᵢ).

The matrix identity gives vᵀΣv=Var(vᵀX)≥0. A covariance matrix is therefore symmetric and positive semidefinite. It need not be positive definite. The earlier joint table with P(A=1,B=1)=0.2, P(A=1,B=0)=0.3 and P(A=0,B=1)=0.1 gives variances 0.25 and 0.21 and covariance 0.05. Thus Σ=[[0.25,0.05],[0.05,0.21]]. These are exact population moments, not an unbiased sample estimate.

The chapter counterexample draws X∼U(−1,1), an independent fair sign S and Y=SX. Since E[S]=0, Cov(X,Y)=0. Yet |Y|=|X|, so the variables remain dependent. The following fourth moment test is an original derivation for that source example.

E[X²]=E[Y²]=1/3; E[X²Y²]=E[X⁴]=1/5 ≠ E[X²]E[Y²]=1/9. E[XY]=E[S]E[X²]=0.

A jointly Gaussian distribution is an important special case. Zero cross covariance then implies independence of the corresponding subvectors. This conclusion uses the joint Gaussian structure. Separate Gaussian marginals are insufficient. A linear correlation check alone does not supply that structure.

The Bernoulli variable in section 3.9.1 on page 60 has numerical states 0 and 1. Its parameter φ=P(X=1) lies in [0,1]. Since X²=X, its mean and variance follow directly. A univariate Gaussian separates location from scale

The Gaussian on pages 61 to 62 uses mean μ and standard deviation σ>0. Its second displayed parameter is the variance σ². The denominator contains the standard deviation rather than the variance. Precision is β=1/σ², not 1/σ. These conventions determine both normalization and the units of the exponent.

N(x;μ,σ²)=[1/(√(2π)σ)]exp[−(x−μ)²/(2σ²)], μ∈ℝ, σ>0. β=σ⁻²>0; N(x;μ,β⁻¹)=√(β/(2π)) exp[−β(x−μ)²/2].

Standardization Z=(X−μ)/σ separates location and scale. An original example uses μ=2, σ=3 and x=5. The standardized value is 1. Its density is exp(−1/2)/(3√(2π))≈0.080657. Substituting variance 9 where the formula expects standard deviation changes both scale and normalization.

The chapter motivates Gaussians through a central limit argument and maximum uncertainty. Conditions matter. The classical standardized sum assumes independent identically distributed variables with finite nonzero variance. Independence alone is insufficient. A Gaussian maximizes differential entropy at fixed mean and variance. This does not make a single symmetric peak suitable for every dataset.

Sigmoid and softplus encode parameter constraints

The sigmoid in section 3.10 on pages 65 to 68 maps finite real values to (0,1). It can parameterize a Bernoulli probability. Softplus maps them to (0,∞), which can parameterize a positive scale or precision. The choice between scale and precision remains a modeling decision. The function does not make these meanings interchangeable.

Continuous variables require measures and volume corrections

Section 3.12 on pages 69 to 70 restricts probability to measurable sets in a chosen sigma algebra. These notes do not redevelop measure theory. A fractal or irregular boundary is not automatically nonmeasurable. Almost everywhere is relative to a specified measure. Densities equal almost everywhere relative to volume define the same distribution.

Under an ordinary continuous density, a volume null set has zero probability. An atomic distribution need not have that property. A point has zero length but can carry all of a Dirac probability measure. This connects the technical discussion to empirical distributions and makes the reference measure essential.

Y=g(X): pᵧ(y)=pₓ(g⁻¹(y))·|d g⁻¹(y)/dy|. Equivalently pₓ(x)=pᵧ(g(x))·|g′(x)|.

The scalar formula uses a differentiable invertible transformation with the appropriate nonzero derivative conditions on the relevant region. It can be interpreted almost everywhere. Corresponding small intervals carry equal mass, rather than equal density values. The absolute value prevents a reversal of orientation from creating negative density.

X∼U(0,1), Y=X/2: pᵧ(y)=2 on (0,1/2), ∫₀¹ᐟ²2dy=1. Omitting the Jacobian gives ∫₀¹ᐟ²1dy=1/2.

The shape effect of KL direction depends on the approximation family

D(P∥Q) penalizes low Q density where P commonly generates samples. D(Q∥P) evaluates the cost where Q places its own mass, penalizing regions where P is small. This difference explains the source illustration. Support, model family and optimization still matter to the resulting shape.

q=N(μ,v), v>0: H(P,q)=½ln(2πv)+Eₚ[(X−μ)²]/(2v). At its finite-variance optimum: μ*=Eₚ[X], v*=Varₚ(X)>0.

The formula is an original derivation for the forward KL projection onto a single Gaussian. Differentiating first in μ matches the mean. Differentiating in v matches the variance. The target needs finite positive variance and a well defined objective. A balanced mixture of N(−m,s²) and N(m,s²) gives μ*=0 and v*=s²+m².

The reverse objective generally has no equivalent moment matching solution. The illustrated symmetric choices depend on the low density valley between components. Closer modes or a multimodal Q can change the result. Choosing a direction expresses a mismatch cost. Neither direction is mathematically more true in every application.

The undirected model on pages 75 to 77 associates nonnegative potentials with cliques. A potential is not a local probability and need not sum to one. Their product needs a finite positive normalizer. Finite nonnegative potentials on a finite state space suffice if at least one configuration is positive.
