  1. 输入：带有基础概率、奖励与安全代价的候选完整回答。奖励与代价是不同量。Input: candidate completions with base probability, reward, and safety cost. Reward and cost are different quantities.

  3. Gibbs 分布以调整分数的指数重新加权基础概率。再计算此分布下的期望代价。A Gibbs distribution reweights base probabilities by the exponentiated adjusted score. Measure expected cost under this distribution.
  4. 当平均代价超过目标时，对偶更新提高 λ。定理需要相应假设；确定性的 best-of-N 选择不等于这种 Gibbs 采样。The dual update increases λ when average cost exceeds the target. The theorem requires its assumptions; deterministic best-of-N selection is not identical to this Gibbs sampling.

校准奖励，而非发明解码器

LARA 在已有奖励中加入经过校准的代价惩罚，并将结果交给推理时对齐程序。

起点目标是在限制期望代价的同时，最大化期望奖励减去 KL 惩罚。

奖励与代价由固定模型给出，其分数不会自动成为真实有用性和危害。

maximize_π E[r] − β E_x KL(π(·|x) || π_ref(·|x)), subject to E[c] ≤ τ

标量对偶问题

在分数有界且存在严格可行策略时，拉格朗日函数将约束转化为乘子 λ ≥ 0。

r_λ = r − λc; π*_λ(y|x) ∝ π_ref(y|x) exp[(r(x,y) − λc(x,y))/β]
g(λ) = β E_x log E_{π_ref} exp[(r − λc)/β] + λτ

求导得到 g′(λ) = τ − E_{π*_λ}[c]，因此期望代价超预算时，最优乘子应向上调整。

二阶导数为条件代价方差的期望除以 β，因此非负。

常数代价不提供曲率，因此强凸性需要额外的方差假设。

搜索区间必须包含有用解；选到区间上边界本身不能证明可行。

校准与有限样本

对每个校准提示词，程序从参考模型采样若干回答，并估计对数配分函数。

然后跨提示词平均，并最小化所得凸标量目标。

有限样本定理假设奖励与代价有界、最优点位于区间内部，且紧区间内强凸。

误差同时包含提示词数量 N 和每个提示词的回答数 K。

K 固定时，仅增加 N 不能消除内部 Monte Carlo 误差。

必须保留对策略不匹配的说明

精确推导针对完整回答上的 Gibbs 分布。

逐词局部奖励修改通常不会采样其精确下一词条件分布，因为后者需要对未来续写求和。

即便普通的确定性 Best-of-N 选择，一般也不等于有限温度 Gibbs 采样。

因此，应直接检查具体解码器的经验约束满足情况，而不是从理想策略公式自动继承保证。

这一限定也适用于论文对序列级重排序的解释。

Calibrate a reward rather than invent a decoder

LARA adds a calibrated cost penalty to an existing reward and passes the result to an inference-time alignment procedure.

Its starting objective maximizes expected reward minus a KL penalty while constraining expected cost.

The reward and cost are fixed models; their scores are not automatically ground-truth helpfulness and harm.

maximize_π E[r] − β E_x KL(π(·|x) || π_ref(·|x)), subject to E[c] ≤ τ

The scalar dual problem

Under bounded scores and a strict feasible policy, the Lagrangian converts the constraint into a multiplier λ ≥ 0.

r_λ = r − λc; π*_λ(y|x) ∝ π_ref(y|x) exp[(r(x,y) − λc(x,y))/β]
g(λ) = β E_x log E_{π_ref} exp[(r − λc)/β] + λτ

Differentiating gives g′(λ) = τ − E_{π*_λ}[c], so excess expected cost pushes the optimal multiplier upward.

The second derivative is the expected conditional cost variance divided by β and is nonnegative.

A constant cost supplies no curvature, so strong convexity needs an additional variance assumption.

The search interval must contain a useful solution; choosing its upper boundary does not itself establish feasibility.

Calibration and finite samples

For each calibration prompt, the procedure samples several responses from the reference model and estimates the log partition function.

It averages across prompts and minimizes the resulting convex scalar objective.

The finite-sample theorem assumes bounded rewards and costs, an interior optimum, and strong convexity on a compact interval.

Its error contains both the number of prompts N and the number of responses per prompt K.

Increasing N alone does not eliminate the inner Monte Carlo error when K stays fixed.

The policy mismatch must stay visible

The exact derivation concerns a Gibbs distribution over complete responses.

A token-local reward modification generally does not sample its exact next-token conditional, which would require summing over future completions.

Even ordinary deterministic Best-of-N selection is not generally identical to finite-temperature Gibbs sampling.

Thus empirical constraint satisfaction by a chosen decoder should be checked directly rather than inherited from the ideal policy formula.

This qualification also applies to the paper's sequence-level reranking interpretation.

Experiments and practical boundaries

The main experiments use Alpaca-finetuned Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen-2.5-3B as reference policies.

Reward and cost models use BeaverTails preference labels, and comparisons include Safe-RLHF, SafeDPO, and inference-time baselines.

Figure 1 sweeps λ to inspect the measured helpfulness-cost tradeoff; an 8B check appears in the appendix.

A calibrated expected-cost budget is a population average, not a guarantee for every prompt or a bound on attack success without a matching cost definition.

A reproduction should separate calibration prompts from evaluation prompts and compare the deployed decoder's cost with the ideal tilted estimate.

Distribution shift, cost-model error, and inaccurate tail estimates remain important limitations.
