Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers for High-Resolution Image Synthesis

Patrick Esser*, Sumith Kulal, Andreas Blattmann, …, Robin Rombach*（Stability AI；* 共同一作）

ICML 2024 · 整理自 arXiv v1（2024 年 3 月 5 日）

三个主要结论
  • 训练目标：rf/lognorm(0.00, 1.00) 在 61 种 formulation 中平均排名 1.54，第一；均匀采样的 rf 排名 5.67，也不如以往常用的 eps/linear（2.88）。
  • 架构：CC12M 上 MM-DiT 的验证损失、CLIP、FID 都优于 DiT、CrossDiT、UViT；三套权重只比两套略好，所以用两套。
  • 放大：depth 15 到 38（8B），训练算力到 5×10²² FLOPs，没有饱和迹象；验证损失与 GenEval（r=−0.920）、人类偏好 Elo（r=−0.982）、T2I-CompBench（r=−0.898）强相关；大模型少步采样时掉点更少。
一句话

把 rectified flow（数据与噪声之间连直线，回归速度 ε−x₀）第一次系统地放大到文生图：训练时多采样中间的 t（logit-normal），在 61 种 formulation 的对比中最好；骨干换成双流权重 + 联合注意力的 MM-DiT；再配上 16 通道 AE、50/50 合成 caption、QK-norm、按分辨率平移 timestep。模型放大到 8B，验证损失平滑下降且与 GenEval、人类偏好强相关；GenEval 总分 0.74，超过 DALL-E 3（0.67）。

问题：rectified flow 理论好，但大规模没人验证过
  • 扩散的 forward 路径该怎么选？路径决定反向 ODE 的弯曲程度：弯的路径要很多步积分；直的路径一步就可能走完、误差累积也少。没把噪声加满的 forward 过程还会造成训练与测试不一致（如生成偏灰的图）。
  • Rectified flow（Liu et al.; Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden; Lipman et al.）用直线连接数据和噪声，理论更好、概念更简单，但只在中小规模、多为类别条件的实验里显示过优势，尚未成为标准做法。
  • 文生图骨干：常见做法把固定的文本表示通过 cross-attention 喂进网络，文本表示本身不随图像更新，作者认为这并不理想；文生图扩散模型的 scaling law 也没人研究过。
方案：三件事
  • 改进 RF 训练：把 t 的分布从均匀改成偏向中间（logit-normal、mode、CosMap），等价于给损失加时间权重；在统一框架下与 EDM、cosine、LDM-Linear 等 61 种 formulation 做大规模对比。
  • MM-DiT：文本 token 和图像 token 各用一套权重，在注意力里拼接，信息双向流动。
  • Scaling 研究 + 高分辨率配方：16 通道 AE、50/50 CogVLM 合成 caption、QK-norm、可变长宽比位置编码、分辨率相关 timestep shift（α=3），最后 DPO 对齐。

统一框架、timestep 分布与 MM-DiT 细节
论文精读 · 统一框架、timestep 分布与 MM-DiT 细节VIP 速查表：Stable Diffusion 3（SD3）arXiv 2403.03206v1
统一框架：一条 forward 路径

生成 ODE：从噪声 p₁ 的样本出发，解生成 ODE 得到数据 p₀ 的样本。直接用可微 ODE 求解器训练（Neural ODE）太贵，所以改为不仿真（simulation-free）地回归一个向量场。

dyₜ = vΘ(yₜ,t) dt

Forward 过程：所有 formulation 都写成

zₜ = aₜx₀ + bₜε，ε∼N(0,I)

边界 a₀=1, b₀=0, a₁=0, b₁=1：t=0 是数据，t=1 是纯噪声。ψₜ(·|ε): x₀↦aₜx₀+bₜε 给出条件路径，其速度 uₜ(z|ε) 生成 pₜ(·|ε)。边缘向量场（Lipman et al.）：

uₜ(z) = Eε[uₜ(z|ε) pₜ(z|ε)/pₜ(z)]

生成边缘路径 pₜ（附录 B.1 用连续性方程证明）。FM → CFM：直接回归 uₜ(z) 不可算（要对 ε 边缘化）：

LFM = Eₜ,pₜ(z) ‖vΘ(z,t)−uₜ(z)‖²

换成条件目标，梯度相同、只差常数（附录 B.1 式 30 到 36）：

LCFM = Eₜ,pₜ(z|ε),p(ε) ‖vΘ(z,t)−uₜ(z|ε)‖²

换成噪声预测：用 log-SNR λₜ 改写条件速度。

uₜ(z|ε) = (a′ₜ/aₜ)z − (λ′ₜbₜ/2)ε；λₜ=log(aₜ²/bₜ²)

定义 εΘ = 2/(λ′ₜbₜ)·((a′ₜ/aₜ)zₜ−vΘ(zₜ,t))，CFM 就变成带权的 ε 预测损失。任何时间加权都不改变最优解，所以统一写成（Kingma & Gao 2023）：

Lw(x₀) = −½ Eₜ,ε[wₜλ′ₜ ‖εΘ(zₜ,t)−ε‖²]

注：不同 formulation 的区别 = (aₜ,bₜ) 的选择 + 网络参数化（ε / v / F / 速度）+ 权重 wₜ，以及下面要讲的 t 的分布。

注意：SD3 的时间方向与 Liu et al. 相反
  • SD3：x₀ 是数据，ε 是噪声；t=0 在数据端、t=1 在噪声端；生成时从 t=1 积分到 t=0。
  • Liu et al. 2022：X₀~π₀ 是噪声、X₁ 是数据，回归 X₁−X₀，从 t=0 积分到 1。对应关系：
Liu: Xₜ=tX₁+(1−t)X₀；SD3: zₜ=(1−t)x₀+tε；tSD3=1−tLiu

所以 SD3 的速度目标 ε−x₀ 指向噪声，与 Liu 的 X₁−X₀ 差一个负号；读公式（尤其 logit-normal 的 m 正负、shift 公式）时要换算。

对比的几类 forward 过程（第 3 节）
  • Rectified Flow：aₜ=1−t, bₜ=t，网络直接输出速度 vΘ，用 LCFM，对应 wₜRF=t/(1−t)。
  • EDM（Karras et al.）：方差爆炸式路径，F 预测：
zₜ=x₀+bₜε；bₜ=exp F⁻¹N(t|Pₘ,Pₛ)

此时 λₜ~N(−2Pₘ,(2Pₛ)²)。原论文参数是 (Pₘ,Pₛ)=(−1.2,1.2)。Cosine（Nichol & Dhariwal）：

zₜ=cos(πt/2)x₀+sin(πt/2)ε
wₜ=sech(λₜ/2)（ε-pred）；wₜ=e^(−λₜ/2)（v-pred）

LDM-Linear（LDM 用的）：DDPM 调度的变体，方差保持，bₜ=√(1−aₜ²)，离散 t=0,…,T−1：

aₜ=(∏ᵢ₌₀^(t−1)(1−βᵢ))^(1/2)；√βᵢ=√β₀+i/(T−1)(√βT−√β₀)

DDPM 则是 βₜ 本身线性。实验里记作 eps/linear、v/linear。

核心改进：给 RF 换 timestep 分布（3.1 节）
  • 动机：RF 损失在 [0,1] 上均匀训练所有 t。但目标 ε−x₀ 在中间最难：t=0 和 t=1 时最优预测只是某个分布的均值，中间要同时“猜”数据和噪声。
  • 采样 = 加权：t 从均匀改为密度 π(t)，等价于权重：
wₜ=t/(1−t)·π(t)

即在 RF 原有权重上再乘 π(t)。Logit-Normal：u~N(m,s)，t=sigmoid(u)：

πln(t;m,s)=[s√(2π)t(1−t)]⁻¹ exp(−(logit(t)−m)²/(2s²))

m<0 偏向数据端，m>0 偏向噪声端；s 控制宽度。缺点：端点 0、1 处密度为零。

Mode（重尾）：为检验“端点密度为零”是否有害，用一个在 [0,1] 上处处为正的密度：u~U(0,1)，t=fmode(u)，密度 πmode(t;s)=d fmode⁻¹(t)/dt：

fmode(u;s)=1−u−s(cos²(πu/2)−1+u)

monotone for −1≤s≤2/(π−2)。s>0 偏向中点，s<0 偏向两端；s=0 就是均匀分布（以往 RF 的默认）。CosMap：让 RF 的 log-SNR 与 cosine 调度一致，u~U(0,1)：

t=1/(1+tan(πu/2))；πCosMap(t)=2/(π−2πt+2πt²)
61 种 formulation 是怎么来的（5.1 节）
  • ε 与 v 预测 × linear / cosine 调度：4 种。
  • rf/mode(s)：s 在 −1 到 1.75 间均匀取 7 个，加 s=1.0 与 s=0（即均匀 rf）：9 种。
  • rf/lognorm(m,s)：m∈[−1,1]、s∈[0.2,2.2] 的网格：30 种。
  • rf/cosmap：1 种。
  • edm(Pₘ,Pₛ)：Pₘ∈[−1.2,1.2]、Pₛ∈[0.6,1.8]：15 种。
  • edm/rf、edm/cos（匹配 rf、v/cos 的 log-SNR 权重）：2 种。
  • 设置：ImageNet（caption “a photo of a 〈class name〉”）与 CC12M；batch 1024，AdamW lr 10⁻⁴，1000 步 warmup；EMA 0.99（每 100 个 batch 更新）。三个文本编码器各以 46.4% 概率置零，约 10% 的步是无条件训练（供 CFG）。
  • 评测：取 EMA 验证损失最小的 checkpoint；COCO-2014 val 上算 CLIP score 与 CLIP L/14 特征上的 FID，每种设置 1000 张。Euler 采样 6 种设置：50 步 × CFG 1.0 / 2.5 / 5.0，以及 5 / 10 / 25 步 × CFG 5.0。

高分辨率配方（5.3 节）
  • 数据：过滤色情内容、低美学分、重复图（SSCD 聚类去重，16,000 个簇；clique 大小 10 时疑似记忆样本减少 5×）。AE latent 和文本嵌入预先算好存盘：T5 要 19.05 GB 显存，在线算会让 2B 模型每步慢 98.3%。
  • 两阶段：先在 256² 预训练，再在更高分辨率、混合长宽比上微调；按像素数≈S² 分桶采样。
  • QK-norm：高分辨率下混合精度训练会发散；改全精度要慢约 2×。对 Q、K 加可学习尺度的 RMSNorm（Dehghani et al. 的 ViT 做法），配合 AdamW ε=10⁻¹⁵，可稳定 bf16 混合精度训练；对没用 QK-norm 的预训练模型也能后加。
  • 位置编码：2D 频率编码。不同长宽比直接插值会弄错边长，所以用“扩展 + 插值”的网格：纵向取 ((p−(hmax−s)/2)·256/S)ₚ，再中心裁剪。

σ(t,n)=t/(1−t)·√(1/n)

令 σ(tₙ,n)=σ(tₘ,m) 解出：

tₘ=√(m/n)tₙ/[1+(√(m/n)−1)tₙ]；α=√(m/n)
λₜₘ=λₜₙ−2logα=λₜₙ−log(m/n)

即 log-SNR 平移 log(m/n)（类似 Hoogeboom et al.）。α>1 时 tₘ>tₙ，同一步更靠噪声端。

Three Main Conclusions

One-Sentence Summary

This work first systematically scales rectified flow to text-to-image generation. Rectified flow connects data and noise with straight lines and regresses velocity ε−x₀. Training samples intermediate t more often through a logit-normal distribution, performing best among 61 formulations. The backbone becomes MM-DiT, with separate stream weights and joint attention. The recipe also includes a 16-channel AE, 50/50 synthetic captions, QK-norm, and resolution-dependent timestep shifts. The model scales to 8B parameters. Validation loss decreases smoothly and strongly correlates with GenEval and human preference. Its overall GenEval score is 0.74, above DALL-E 3 at 0.67.

Problem: Promising RF Theory Without Large-Scale Validation
  • How should the forward diffusion path be chosen? This path determines the curvature of the reverse ODE. Curved paths require many integration steps. Straight paths may be traversed in one step with less accumulated error. A forward process that fails to reach full noise also creates training-test mismatch, such as gray-looking generated images.
  • Rectified flow (Liu et al.; Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden; Lipman et al.) connects data and noise with straight lines. Its theory is more favorable and its concept simpler. However, its advantages had appeared only in small- to medium-scale experiments, mostly with class conditioning. It had not become standard practice.
  • Text-to-image backbone: A common design feeds fixed text representations into the network through cross-attention. The text representation itself does not update with the image. The authors consider this suboptimal. Scaling laws for text-to-image diffusion models had also not been studied.
Approach: Three Components
  • Improved RF training: The distribution of t changes from uniform to intermediate-biased sampling through logit-normal, mode, and CosMap. This is equivalent to time-dependent loss weighting. A unified framework supports large-scale comparisons of 61 formulations, including EDM, cosine, and LDM-Linear.
  • MM-DiT: Text and image tokens have separate weight sets. They concatenate inside attention, allowing information to flow in both directions.
  • Scaling study and high-resolution recipe: A 16-channel AE, 50/50 CogVLM-generated captions, QK-norm, and variable-aspect-ratio positional embeddings are combined. Resolution-dependent timestep shifts use α=3. DPO alignment follows.

Unified Framework, Timestep Distributions, and MM-DiT Details

Unified Framework: One Forward Path

Generative ODE: A sample starts from noise distribution p₁. Solving the generative ODE produces a sample from data distribution p₀. Direct training through a differentiable ODE solver, as in Neural ODEs, is too expensive. The alternative regresses a vector field without simulation.

dyₜ = vΘ(yₜ,t) dt

Forward process: Every formulation takes the following form.

zₜ = aₜx₀ + bₜε，ε∼N(0,I)

The boundary values are a₀=1, b₀=0, a₁=0, and b₁=1. Thus t=0 represents data and t=1 pure noise. The map ψₜ(·|ε): x₀↦aₜx₀+bₜε defines the conditional path. Its velocity uₜ(z|ε) generates pₜ(·|ε). The marginal vector field follows Lipman et al.:

uₜ(z) = Eε[uₜ(z|ε) pₜ(z|ε)/pₜ(z)]

This generates marginal path pₜ, as proved using the continuity equation in Appendix B.1. FM → CFM: Direct regression of uₜ(z) is intractable because ε must be marginalized:

LFM = Eₜ,pₜ(z) ‖vΘ(z,t)−uₜ(z)‖²

Replacing it with the conditional target preserves the gradients and changes the objective only by a constant. The derivation appears in Appendix B.1, Equations 30 to 36:

LCFM = Eₜ,pₜ(z|ε),p(ε) ‖vΘ(z,t)−uₜ(z|ε)‖²

Noise prediction: The conditional velocity can be rewritten using log-SNR λₜ.

uₜ(z|ε) = (a′ₜ/aₜ)z − (λ′ₜbₜ/2)ε；λₜ=log(aₜ²/bₜ²)

The definition εΘ=2/(λ′ₜbₜ)·((a′ₜ/aₜ)zₜ−vΘ(zₜ,t)) turns CFM into a weighted ε-prediction loss. Any time weighting leaves the optimal solution unchanged. The unified expression follows Kingma & Gao (2023):

Lw(x₀) = −½ Eₜ,ε[wₜλ′ₜ ‖εΘ(zₜ,t)−ε‖²]

Note: Formulations differ through the choice of (aₜ,bₜ), network parameterization (ε / v / F / velocity), and weight wₜ. The distribution of t, discussed below, provides another distinction.

Note: SD3 Reverses the Time Direction of Liu et al.
  • SD3: x₀ is data and ε is noise. Time t=0 is the data endpoint, while t=1 is noise. Generation integrates from t=1 to t=0.
  • Liu et al. 2022: X₀~π₀ is noise and X₁ is data. The target is X₁−X₀, with integration from t=0 to 1. The correspondence is:
Liu: Xₜ=tX₁+(1−t)X₀；SD3: zₜ=(1−t)x₀+tε；tSD3=1−tLiu

SD3 therefore predicts ε−x₀ toward noise, differing in sign from Liu's X₁−X₀. The formulas require conversion between conventions. This particularly affects the sign of logit-normal parameter m and the shift formula.

Compared Forward Processes (Section 3)
  • Rectified Flow: aₜ=1−t and bₜ=t. The network directly predicts velocity vΘ using LCFM, with corresponding weight wₜRF=t/(1−t).
  • EDM (Karras et al.): A variance-exploding path with F prediction:
zₜ=x₀+bₜε；bₜ=exp F⁻¹N(t|Pₘ,Pₛ)

Here λₜ~N(−2Pₘ,(2Pₛ)²). The original paper uses (Pₘ,Pₛ)=(−1.2,1.2). Cosine follows Nichol & Dhariwal:

zₜ=cos(πt/2)x₀+sin(πt/2)ε
wₜ=sech(λₜ/2)（ε-pred）；wₜ=e^(−λₜ/2)（v-pred）

LDM-Linear, used in LDM, modifies the DDPM schedule. It preserves variance, with bₜ=√(1−aₜ²) and discrete t=0,…,T−1:

aₜ=(∏ᵢ₌₀^(t−1)(1−βᵢ))^(1/2)；√βᵢ=√β₀+i/(T−1)(√βT−√β₀)

In DDPM, βₜ itself is linear. The experiments label these formulations eps/linear and v/linear.

Core Change: A New RF Timestep Distribution (Section 3.1)
  • Motivation: The RF loss trains uniformly over t in [0,1]. However, predicting ε−x₀ is hardest in the middle. At t=0 and t=1, the optimal prediction is merely a distribution mean. Intermediate times require simultaneously inferring data and noise.
  • Sampling equals weighting: Replacing uniform t with density π(t) gives an equivalent weight:
wₜ=t/(1−t)·π(t)

The original RF weight is therefore multiplied by π(t). Logit-Normal uses u~N(m,s) and t=sigmoid(u):

πln(t;m,s)=[s√(2π)t(1−t)]⁻¹ exp(−(logit(t)−m)²/(2s²))

Values m<0 favor the data endpoint, while m>0 favor noise. Parameter s controls the width. One drawback is zero density at endpoints 0 and 1.

Mode, a heavy-tailed distribution, tests whether zero endpoint density is harmful. It uses a density positive throughout [0,1]. With u~U(0,1), t=fmode(u), and πmode(t;s)=d fmode⁻¹(t)/dt:

fmode(u;s)=1−u−s(cos²(πu/2)−1+u)

The function is monotone for −1≤s≤2/(π−2). Positive s favors the middle, and negative s favors both ends. At s=0, the distribution is uniform, matching the previous RF default. CosMap matches RF log-SNR to the cosine schedule, with u~U(0,1):

t=1/(1+tan(πu/2))；πCosMap(t)=2/(π−2πt+2πt²)
The 61 Formulations (Section 5.1)
  • ε and v prediction × linear / cosine schedules: 4 formulations.
  • rf/mode(s): Seven evenly spaced s values from −1 to 1.75, plus s=1.0 and s=0. The latter is uniform rf, giving 9 formulations.
  • rf/lognorm(m,s): A grid over m∈[−1,1] and s∈[0.2,2.2], giving 30 formulations.
  • rf/cosmap: 1 formulation.
  • edm(Pₘ,Pₛ): Pₘ∈[−1.2,1.2] and Pₛ∈[0.6,1.8], giving 15 formulations.
  • edm/rf and edm/cos match the log-SNR weights of rf and v/cos, giving 2 formulations.
  • Setup: ImageNet uses captions of the form “a photo of a 〈class name〉”. CC12M is also evaluated. Training uses batch 1024, AdamW with learning rate 10⁻⁴, and 1000 warmup steps. EMA is 0.99, updated every 100 batches. Each text encoder is independently zeroed with probability 46.4%. About 10% of steps are unconditional, supporting CFG.
  • Evaluation: The checkpoint with the lowest EMA validation loss is selected. COCO-2014 validation provides CLIP scores and FID using CLIP L/14 features, with 1000 images per setting. Six Euler sampling settings are used: 50 steps × CFG 1.0 / 2.5 / 5.0, plus 5 / 10 / 25 steps × CFG 5.0.
  • Ranking: Six sampling settings × EMA or no EMA × 2 datasets gives 24 settings. Within each setting, repeated Pareto-front extraction produces nondominated rankings over (CLIP,FID). The 24 ranks are then averaged.
MM-DiT Details (Section 4, Appendix B.2)
  • Text: CLIP L/14, OpenCLIP bigG/14, and T5-v1.1-XXL are all frozen. Their pooled outputs concatenate into cvec∈R²⁰⁴⁸. The CLIP penultimate-layer sequence has shape 77×2048 and is zero-padded to 4096 dimensions. Concatenation with the T5 sequence, 77×4096, gives cctxt∈R¹⁵⁴ˣ⁴⁰⁹⁶.
  • Why two uses: Pooled vectors preserve only coarse information. They therefore provide modulation together with t, like DiT class conditioning. Fine details require the token sequence.
  • Size depends only on depth d: hidden=64·d, MLP width=4·64·d, and attention heads=d. Depth d=24 gives about 2B parameters, while d=38 gives 8B.

High-Resolution Recipe (Section 5.3)
  • Data: Filtering removes sexual content, low-aesthetic-score images, and duplicates. SSCD clustering uses 16,000 clusters. At clique size 10, suspected memorized samples decrease 5×. AE latents and text embeddings are precomputed and stored. T5 requires 19.05 GB of GPU memory. Computing it online would slow each 2B-model step by 98.3%.
  • Two stages: Pretraining starts at 256². Fine-tuning then uses higher resolutions and mixed aspect ratios, with buckets sampled by pixel count approximately S².
  • QK-norm: Mixed-precision training diverges at high resolution, while full precision would be about 2× slower. Q and K receive RMSNorm with a learnable scale, following Dehghani et al.'s ViT approach. Together with AdamW ε=10⁻¹⁵, this stabilizes bf16 training. QK-norm can also be added to pretrained models that lacked it.
  • Positional encoding: The model uses 2D frequency encoding. Direct interpolation across aspect ratios gives incorrect side lengths. An “extend and interpolate” grid instead uses ((p−(hmax−s)/2)·256/S)ₚ vertically, followed by center cropping.
  • Resolution-dependent timestep shift: More pixels preserve more signal at the same t, requiring more noise. For a constant image, each pixel is zₜ=(1−t)c+tε. Uncertainty in estimating c from n pixels is:
σ(t,n)=t/(1−t)·√(1/n)

Equating σ(tₙ,n) and σ(tₘ,m) gives:

tₘ=√(m/n)tₙ/[1+(√(m/n)−1)tₙ]；α=√(m/n)
λₜₘ=λₜₙ−2logα=λₜₙ−log(m/n)

Thus log-SNR shifts by log(m/n), similarly to Hoogeboom et al. When α>1, tₘ>tₙ, placing the same step closer to the noise endpoint.

DPO alignment (Appendix C) follows Wallace et al.'s Diffusion-DPO. Rank-128 LoRA adapters are added to every linear layer. The 2B model trains for 4k steps, and the 8B model for 2k steps. Human evaluation on 128 Parti prompts shows greater preference for both prompt following and image quality.

Relation to Liu et al. and InstaFlow
  • Inherited from Liu et al. 2022: Straight-line interpolation and regression of straight-line velocity. SD3 uses only 1-rectified flow. RF, Flow Matching, and Stochastic Interpolants are treated as the same model family.
  • Not used: There is no reflow or distillation. Sampling of t is no longer uniform, and time runs in the opposite direction. The derivation follows Lipman's CFM rather than Liu's Rectify theory. Appendix A also notes that RF does not provide optimal transport.
  • InstaFlow (Liu et al. 2023) uses reflow to distill pretrained SD into a few-step or one-step model. It is post-training acceleration. SD3 instead trains a large model from scratch with the RF objective. It targets good quality at few steps rather than one-step generation. The two approaches can be combined.
Questions After Reading
  • 1. What is SD3's “RF” loss? With zₜ=(1−t)x₀+tε, the model regresses vΘ≈ε−x₀ and samples t~logit-normal(0,1).
  • 2. Why does nonuniform t equal weighting? Eₜ~π[ℓₜ]=Eₜ~U[π(t)ℓₜ], with wₜ=t/(1−t)·π(t). The optimum stays unchanged; only the optimization trajectory changes.
  • 3. Why favor intermediate t? At either endpoint, the optimal prediction is merely a mean. Intermediate times are hardest. Uniform rf ranks only 10th among the 16 formulations listed in Table 1.
  • 4. Which formulation is chosen, and why? rf/lognorm(0.00,1.00) averages rank 1.54 across 24 settings. It ranks near the top at both 5 and 50 steps.
  • 5. How does MM-DiT fundamentally differ from CrossDiT? Text has its own weights and updates across layers. Joint attention enables interaction in both directions. In CrossDiT, text supplies only fixed K/V.
  • 6. Why is a timestep shift necessary? At higher resolution, the signal is easier to recover at the same t. Equating σ yields the tₘ formula. The 1024² model uses α=3.
  • 7. What does QK-norm solve? Large models at high resolution suffer exploding attention logits and entropy collapse, causing divergence. RMSNorm enables bf16 training.
  • 8. Why a 16-channel AE? It raises the reconstruction ceiling, with FID 1.06 versus 2.41. It requires a larger model, fitting the scaling objective.
  • 9. Can validation loss replace evaluation? The paper considers it a simple, general indicator. Correlations with GenEval and human preference reach −0.92 and −0.98.
  • 10. Is T5 worthwhile? It has 4.7B parameters and high memory costs. Removing it leaves aesthetics unchanged but reduces the typography win rate to 38%.

三个主要结果
  • reflow 是蒸馏成功的关键（SD 1.4 初步实验，表 1）：直接蒸馏 SD 得到 FID-5k 40.9；先 reflow 再蒸馏得到 31.0，同时胜过 progressive distillation 一步的 37.2，训练只花约 24.65 A100 天。

  • 2-Rectified Flow 本身就是好用的少步模型：步数≤4 时明显好于 SD 1.5 + DPM-Solver（图 9A），且一步模型的隐空间与它对齐，可兼容 SD 的 ControlNet。
一句话

InstaFlow 用纯监督学习把 Stable Diffusion 变成一步文生图模型：先做文本条件 reflow，把 SD 弯曲的概率流 ODE 拉直、改善“噪声 ↔ 图像”的配对（coupling），再把拉直后的 ODE 蒸馏成一次前向。没有对抗训练。InstaFlow-0.9B 生成一张 512×512 图只要 0.09 s，MS COCO 2014 上 FID-30k 13.1（StyleGAN-T：13.9，0.1 s）；MS COCO 2017 上 FID-5k 23.4（此前最好的一步 SD，progressive distillation：37.2）。训练总共 199 A100 GPU 天。

背景：大规模文生图一步生成还没人做成
  • SD 采样慢：SD 是隐空间里的 DDPM，通常要 100 步以上；DPM-Solver 等后处理采样器把它变成保边缘分布的概率流 ODE，可降到约 20 步，但少于 10 步时质量明显下降。
  • 蒸馏到一步失败：progressive distillation 能把 SD 压到 2 至 4 步，但一步 FID-5k 只有 37.2；作者自己直接蒸馏 SD 也“complete failure”（图 7）。
  • 现有一步大模型都是 GAN：StyleGAN-T、GigaGAN 靠对抗训练，生成器和判别器都要精细调参，训练成本 1792 / 4783 A100 天。
核心问题：直接蒸馏为什么失败？
  • SD 的概率流 ODE 轨迹是弯的，它定义的映射 X₀↦ODE[vSD](X₀) 对应一个“差”的噪声 ↔ 图像耦合：相近的噪声可能落到很不同的图，学生网络一步很难模仿。
  • 调超参救不了：9 组学习率 × weight decay 网格搜索，FID-5k 仍在 44 左右（附录表 3）。
  • 解法：先用 reflow 换一个更直、传输代价更低的耦合（边缘分布不变），再蒸馏。

只用最小二乘回归 + LPIPS：没有 GAN 损失，也不需要任何真实图像（训练数据只是文本提示词 + 教师模型生成的配对）。

沿用 Liu et al. 的约定：X₀~π₀ 是噪声（SD 隐空间 4×64×64 的高斯），X₁~π₁ 是图像（的 latent），时间从 t=0（噪声）走到 t=1（图像）。

InstaFlow: Xₜ=(1−t)X₀+tX₁；SD3: zₜ=(1−t)x₀+tε

注：和 SD3 正好相反：本文 t=0 噪声、t=1 图像；SD3 里 t=0 是数据、t=1 是噪声，速度场方向也相反。读两篇时别混。

其余符号：𝒯 文本提示词；D𝒯 提示词数据集（laion2B-en 子集）；vₖ 第 k 个速度场；ODE[v](X₀|𝒯) 从 X₀ 解 ODE 到 t=1 的终点；𝔻(·,·) 图像相似度损失（LPIPS 或 L2）；α CFG 系数。

Rectified flow 回顾（Sec 2.2）

ODE 生成：学速度场 v，使

dZₜ/dt=v(Zₜ,t)，Z₀∼π₀ → Z₁∼π₁

训练目标（Eq 2）：γ 是 (π₀,π₁) 的任意耦合（最简单是独立配对 π₀×π₁），Xₜ 是任意可导插值：

minᵥ E(X₀,X₁)∼γ[∫₀¹ ‖dXₜ/dt−v(Xₜ,t)‖²dt]；Xₜ=ϕ(X₀,X₁,t)

DDIM / 概率流 ODE 对应 Xₜ=αₜX₀+βₜX₁ 的特殊选择（轨迹弯）；rectified flow 选直线插值（Eq 3）：

Xₜ=(1−t)X₀+tX₁ ⇒ dXₜ/dt=X₁−X₀

为什么要“直”：实际用 Euler 法求解（Eq 4），步长 1/N，t∈{0,…,N−1}/N：

Zₜ₊₁/N=Zₜ+(1/N)v(Zₜ,t)

若轨迹是匀速直线

Zₜ=tZ₁+(1−t)Z₀=Z₀+t v(Z₀,0)

则 N=1 一步 Euler 就是精确解（图 4）。所以“拉直轨迹”是降低推理成本的根本办法。

直线度：

S(Z)=∫₀¹ E[‖(Z₁−Z₀)−v(Zₜ,t)‖²]dt

S(Z) 越小越直，全部直线时为 0。

方法①：文本条件 reflow（Eq 5）
vₖ₊₁=argminᵥ ∫₀¹ Eₓ₀,𝒯 ‖(X₁−X₀)−v(Xₜ,t|𝒯)‖²dt
X₁=ODE[vₖ](X₀|𝒯)；Xₜ=tX₁+(1−t)X₀
ODE[vₖ](X₀|𝒯)=X₀+∫₀¹ vₖ(Xₜ,t|𝒯)dt
  • 一句话：用上一个流 vₖ 给每对（噪声，提示词）生成图像，得到配对 (X₀,X₁)，再用直线插值和 Eq 2 的损失训练 vₖ₊₁（多了文本条件 𝒯）。
  • 起点 v₁=vSD：直接把预训练 SD 的概率流 ODE 当作“1-flow”。所以 2-Rectified Flow 是第一次 reflow 的结果，不是 reflow 两次。
  • 离线配对：配对可以事先生成：ODE[vSD] 用 25 步 DPM-Solver、guidance scale 6.0（SD 1.4）或 5.0（SD 1.5，因为 6.0 会让 2-RF 过饱和）。每份数据 1,600,000 对。
# Algorithm 1（整理）：从 SD 训练文本条件 RF
v[1] = v_SD                    # 预训练 SD
for k in 1, 2, ...:            # 上限自定
    v[k+1] = copy(v[k])        # 从 v_k 初始化
    # 配对可离线生成
    pairs = [(x0, T, ODE[v[k]](x0|T))]
    for step:
        x0, T, x1 = sample(pairs)
        t = uniform(0, 1)
        xt = t*x1 + (1-t)*x0
        loss = |(x1-x0) - v[k+1](xt, t|T)|^2
        sgd(loss)
# v[k] 称为 k-Rectified Flow

reflow 的三条性质（来自原 RF 理论）：(1) ODE[vₖ₊₁] 与 ODE[vₖ] 的终点分布相同，vₖ 能生成正确分布则 vₖ₊₁ 也能；(2) 轨迹更直，需要的 Euler 步数更少；若 vₖ₊₁=vₖ（不动点）则轨迹严格是直线；(3) 对所有凸函数 c，传输代价不增：

E[c(ODE[vₖ₊₁](X₀|𝒯)−X₀)] ≤ E[c(ODE[vₖ](X₀|𝒯)−X₀)]

所以新耦合“可能更容易被学生网络学会”。实现：reflow 不改网络结构，只微调 SD 的 U-Net；学习率 10⁻⁶，AdamW，EMA 0.9999，梯度裁剪到 1，warm-up 1,000 步，BF16；文本编码器与 VAE 解码器冻结。

方法②：文本条件蒸馏（Eq 6）

为什么还要蒸馏：理论上要无穷次 reflow 才严格直；reflow 太多次又贵、又累积优化和统计误差。好在一两次 reflow 后就“接近直”，此时再蒸馏：

ṽₖ=argminᵥ Eₓ₀,𝒯 𝔻(ODE[vₖ](X₀|𝒯),X₀+v(X₀|𝒯))

学一个单步 Euler 映射 x↦x+v(x|𝒯)（网络时间固定为 t=0），用可导的图像相似度 𝔻 去逼近 X₀↦ODE[vₖ](X₀|𝒯)。论文的 𝔻 是 LPIPS（AlexNet 版，官方 0.1.4）；放大规模时先 L2 再换 LPIPS。

# Algorithm 2（整理）：蒸馏 k-RF 成一步模型
v_s = copy(v[k])
pairs = [(x0, T, ODE[v[k]](x0|T))]
for step:
    x0, T, x1 = sample(pairs)
    loss = D(x1, x0 + v_s(x0, t=0|T))
    sgd(loss)
# v_s 称为 k-Rectified Flow+Distill

不需要对抗训练，也不需要可逆网络（normalizing flow）。

reflow 与蒸馏是正交的

蒸馏：忠实复制已有映射 X₀↦ODE[vₖ](X₀|𝒯)，映射本身难学就学不好。

reflow：产生一个新的映射 ODE[vₖ₊₁]，因凸传输代价更低而更规则、更平滑。reflow 在形式上是蒸馏前的可选步骤，两者互不替代；但实践中作者发现蒸馏前必须先 reflow。

CFG 速度场（Eq 7）
vᵅ(Zₜ,t|𝒯)=αv(Zₜ,t|𝒯)+(1−α)v(Zₜ,t|NULL)

作用：仿照 classifier-free guidance，在文本条件 RF 上做外推。α 在多样性与质量之间权衡；α=1 时退回原速度场。论文对 2-RF 生成蒸馏数据时取 α=1.5。

注：两个 guidance 别混：SD 教师生成 reflow 数据时用 SD 自己的 guidance scale（6.0 / 5.0）；α 是 2-RF 采样时的系数。

最可能被问的三个问题
  • reflow 不改分布，凭什么帮蒸馏？它改的是配对：同样的边缘分布，换成更直、凸代价更低的噪声 ↔ 图像映射，学生更容易拟合。
  • 训练要真实图片吗？不要。D𝒯 只有提示词，X₁ 全由教师 ODE 生成。
  • 为什么 t 固定为 0？一步模型只在起点调用一次网络，所以蒸馏只用 t=0 这一项。

InstaFlow: One Step is Enough for High-Quality Diffusion-Based Text-to-Image Generation

Xingchao Liu, Xiwen Zhang, Jianzhu Ma, Jian Peng, Qiang Liu (UT Austin; Helixon Research)

ICLR 2024 · Notes based on arXiv v2 (March 23, 2024)

Author code

Three main results
  • Reflow is central to successful distillation in the preliminary SD 1.4 experiments (Table 1). Direct SD distillation gives FID-5k 40.9. Reflow followed by distillation gives 31.0. This also improves on one-step progressive distillation at 37.2. Training costs about 24.65 A100 days.
  • At larger scale, performance reaches the GAN regime (SD 1.5, Table 2). InstaFlow-0.9B gives FID-5k 23.4 and FID-30k 13.10 at 0.09 s. InstaFlow-1.7B, with a Stacked U-Net, gives FID-5k 22.4 and FID-30k 11.83 at 0.12 s.
  • 2-Rectified Flow is itself a useful few-step model. With ≤4 steps, it clearly outperforms SD 1.5 + DPM-Solver (Figure 9A). Its latent space also aligns with the one-step model. This supports compatibility with SD ControlNet.
Central idea

InstaFlow turns Stable Diffusion into a one-step text-to-image model through supervised learning alone. Text-conditioned reflow first straightens SD's curved probability-flow ODE and improves the noise ↔ image coupling. Distillation then compresses the straighter ODE into one forward pass. Training has no adversarial component. InstaFlow-0.9B generates a 512×512 image in 0.09 s. Its MS COCO 2014 FID-30k is 13.1, compared with StyleGAN-T at 13.9 and 0.1 s. On MS COCO 2017, its FID-5k is 23.4. The earlier best one-step SD baseline, progressive distillation, gives 37.2. Total training costs 199 A100 GPU days.

Background: The missing large-scale one-step text-to-image model
  • SD sampling is slow. SD is a DDPM in latent space and typically needs more than 100 steps. Post-hoc samplers such as DPM-Solver express sampling through a probability-flow ODE that preserves the marginals. This reduces sampling to about 20 steps. Quality degrades noticeably below 10 steps.
  • Distillation to one step had failed. Progressive distillation compresses SD to 2 to 4 steps, but its one-step FID-5k is 37.2. The authors also describe their direct SD distillation as a “complete failure” (Figure 7).
  • The existing large one-step models in this comparison are GANs. StyleGAN-T and GigaGAN use adversarial training. Both generator and discriminator need careful tuning. Their training costs are 1792 / 4783 A100 days.
The central problem: Why direct distillation fails
  • SD's probability-flow ODE follows curved trajectories. Its map X₀↦ODE[vSD](X₀) gives a poor noise ↔ image coupling. Nearby noise inputs can map to very different images. A student network struggles to imitate this map in one step.
  • Hyperparameter tuning does not resolve the problem. A grid search over 9 learning-rate × weight-decay combinations still gives FID-5k around 44 (Appendix Table 3).
  • Reflow first replaces this coupling with a straighter one that has lower transport cost. The marginal distributions stay unchanged. Distillation follows this change.

Training uses only least-squares regression and LPIPS. It has no GAN loss and needs no real images. The training data consist of text prompts and pairs generated by the teacher model.

Notation

The notation follows Liu et al. X₀~π₀ is noise, represented by a Gaussian in SD's 4×64×64 latent space. X₁~π₁ is the image latent. Time runs from t=0 at noise to t=1 at the image.

InstaFlow: Xₜ=(1−t)X₀+tX₁；SD3: zₜ=(1−t)x₀+tε

This convention is the reverse of SD3. Here, t=0 is noise and t=1 is the image. In SD3, t=0 is data and t=1 is noise. The velocity-field direction is also reversed. The two papers therefore use different endpoint conventions.

Other symbols: 𝒯 is the text prompt. D𝒯 is the prompt dataset, a subset of laion2B-en. vₖ is the kth velocity field. ODE[v](X₀|𝒯) is the endpoint obtained by solving the ODE from X₀ to t=1. 𝔻(·,·) is the image-similarity loss, either LPIPS or L2. α is the CFG coefficient.

Rectified flow background (Section 2.2)

ODE generation learns a velocity field v with the following dynamics:

dZₜ/dt=v(Zₜ,t)，Z₀∼π₀ → Z₁∼π₁

In the training objective (Equation 2), γ is any coupling of (π₀,π₁). The simplest choice pairs the endpoints independently through π₀×π₁. Xₜ is any differentiable interpolation:

minᵥ E(X₀,X₁)∼γ[∫₀¹ ‖dXₜ/dt−v(Xₜ,t)‖²dt]；Xₜ=ϕ(X₀,X₁,t)

DDIM and probability-flow ODEs correspond to particular choices of Xₜ=αₜX₀+βₜX₁, with curved trajectories. Rectified flow uses linear interpolation instead (Equation 3):

Xₜ=(1−t)X₀+tX₁ ⇒ dXₜ/dt=X₁−X₀

Straightness matters because sampling uses the Euler method (Equation 4). The step size is 1/N, with t∈{0,…,N−1}/N:

Zₜ₊₁/N=Zₜ+(1/N)v(Zₜ,t)

For a constant-speed straight trajectory,

Zₜ=tZ₁+(1−t)Z₀=Z₀+t v(Z₀,0)

a single Euler step with N=1 is exact (Figure 4). Straightening trajectories therefore provides a fundamental way to reduce inference cost.

Straightness measure:

S(Z)=∫₀¹ E[‖(Z₁−Z₀)−v(Zₜ,t)‖²]dt

A smaller S(Z) means straighter trajectories. S(Z) is 0 when all trajectories are straight.

Method 1: Text-conditioned reflow (Equation 5)
vₖ₊₁=argminᵥ ∫₀¹ Eₓ₀,𝒯 ‖(X₁−X₀)−v(Xₜ,t|𝒯)‖²dt
X₁=ODE[vₖ](X₀|𝒯)；Xₜ=tX₁+(1−t)X₀
ODE[vₖ](X₀|𝒯)=X₀+∫₀¹ vₖ(Xₜ,t|𝒯)dt
  • The previous flow vₖ generates an image for each noise-prompt pair. This gives paired endpoints (X₀,X₁). The next field vₖ₊₁ is trained on their linear interpolations with the loss in Equation 2 and the additional text condition 𝒯.
  • The starting point is v₁=vSD. The pretrained SD probability-flow ODE serves directly as the “1-flow”. Thus, 2-Rectified Flow is the result of the first reflow operation. It does not mean two reflow operations.
  • Pair generation can happen offline. ODE[vSD] uses 25-step DPM-Solver with guidance scale 6.0 for SD 1.4 or 5.0 for SD 1.5. A scale of 6.0 oversaturates 2-RF in the latter setting. Each dataset contains 1,600,000 pairs.
# Algorithm 1 (reorganized): Text-conditioned RF training from SD
v[1] = v_SD                    # Pretrained SD
for k in 1, 2, ...:            # A chosen iteration limit
    v[k+1] = copy(v[k])        # Initialization from v_k
    # Pairs may be generated offline
    pairs = [(x0, T, ODE[v[k]](x0|T))]
    for step:
        x0, T, x1 = sample(pairs)
        t = uniform(0, 1)
        xt = t*x1 + (1-t)*x0
        loss = |(x1-x0) - v[k+1](xt, t|T)|^2
        sgd(loss)
# v[k] is called k-Rectified Flow

The original RF theory gives three properties of reflow. (1) ODE[vₖ₊₁] and ODE[vₖ] have the same endpoint distribution. If vₖ generates the correct distribution, vₖ₊₁ does too. (2) Trajectories become straighter and require fewer Euler steps. At a fixed point, vₖ₊₁=vₖ, the trajectories are exactly straight. (3) Transport cost does not increase for any convex function c:

E[c(ODE[vₖ₊₁](X₀|𝒯)−X₀)] ≤ E[c(ODE[vₖ](X₀|𝒯)−X₀)]

The new coupling may therefore be easier for the student network to learn. Reflow keeps the architecture unchanged and only fine-tunes SD's U-Net. The settings are a learning rate of 10⁻⁶, AdamW, EMA 0.9999, gradient clipping at 1, a 1,000-step warm-up, and BF16. The text encoder and VAE decoder remain frozen.

Method 2: Text-conditioned distillation (Equation 6)

Distillation is still useful because exact straightness theoretically requires infinitely many reflow operations. Repeated reflow is expensive and accumulates optimization and statistical errors. After one or two reflow operations, the trajectories are already close to straight. Distillation then uses the following objective:

ṽₖ=argminᵥ Eₓ₀,𝒯 𝔻(ODE[vₖ](X₀|𝒯),X₀+v(X₀|𝒯))

The student learns the one-step Euler map x↦x+v(x|𝒯). Its network time is fixed at t=0. A differentiable image-similarity loss 𝔻 fits the teacher map X₀↦ODE[vₖ](X₀|𝒯). The paper uses LPIPS with AlexNet from the official 0.1.4 implementation. At larger scale, training starts with L2 and then switches to LPIPS.

# Algorithm 2 (reorganized): Distillation of k-RF into a one-step model
v_s = copy(v[k])
pairs = [(x0, T, ODE[v[k]](x0|T))]
for step:
    x0, T, x1 = sample(pairs)
    loss = D(x1, x0 + v_s(x0, t=0|T))
    sgd(loss)
# v_s is called k-Rectified Flow+Distill

Neither adversarial training nor an invertible normalizing-flow network is required.

Reflow and distillation are orthogonal

Distillation faithfully reproduces an existing map, X₀↦ODE[vₖ](X₀|𝒯). If that map is difficult to learn, the student approximation remains poor.

Reflow creates a new map, ODE[vₖ₊₁]. Its lower convex transport cost gives a more regular and smoother correspondence. Formally, reflow is optional before distillation. The two operations do not replace each other. In practice, the authors find that reflow is necessary before successful distillation.

The CFG velocity field (Equation 7)
vᵅ(Zₜ,t|𝒯)=αv(Zₜ,t|𝒯)+(1−α)v(Zₜ,t|NULL)

This field extrapolates text-conditioned RF in the style of classifier-free guidance. α controls the tradeoff between diversity and quality. At α=1, it reduces to the original velocity field. The paper uses α=1.5 when 2-RF generates distillation data.

The two guidance settings have different roles. The SD teacher uses its own guidance scale, 6.0 / 5.0, to generate reflow data. α is the coefficient used when sampling 2-RF.

Connections to the original RF paper and SD3
  • The original Rectified Flow paper (Liu et al., ICLR 2023) comes from the same team and provides the same theory of linear interpolation, reflow, and distillation. It tests small datasets such as CIFAR-10, where one-step 2-RF+Distill reaches FID 4.85. Its 1-RF is trained from scratch with linear interpolation. InstaFlow instead uses SD's probability-flow ODE directly as the 1-flow. It adds text conditioning, the CFG velocity field in Equation 7, large offline pairing datasets, and a Stacked U-Net. The earlier observation that one reflow operation is usually enough also holds here. The 3-RF results are mixed (Table 4).
  • SD3 (Esser et al., 2024) trains a large MM-DiT model from scratch with an RF objective. It studies timestep sampling and architectural scaling. Sampling still uses multiple steps, without reflow or distillation. InstaFlow fine-tunes an existing SD model for one-step generation. SD3 addresses the flow that is trained. InstaFlow addresses the compression of a flow into one step. Their notation uses opposite endpoint conventions (page 2).
Three likely discussion questions
  • Reflow helps distillation despite preserving the distribution. It changes the pairing. The same marginals admit a straighter noise ↔ image map with lower convex transport cost. The student can fit this map more easily.
  • Training needs no real images. D𝒯 contains only prompts, and the teacher ODE generates every X₁.
  • The network time is fixed at t=0. The one-step model calls the network only at the starting point. Distillation therefore uses only t=0.

Interpretation

InstaFlow-0.9B and one-step PD have comparable distillation costs, at 108 versus 108.8 A100 days. Their FID-5k values are 23.4 versus 37.2. Within the 0.1 s regime, InstaFlow's 13.10 improves on StyleGAN-T's 13.90. It still trails GigaGAN at 9.09 and 0.13 s, as well as multi-step diffusion models.

Experimental setup (Section 3.1)

The model starts from SD 1.4 and uses batch size 32, 8 A100 GPUs, and AdamW. Direct distillation generates 32×100,000=3,200,000 pairs and trains for 100,000 steps. For a matched training cost, 2-RF trains for 50,000 steps and its student for another 50,000 steps. The distillation dataset contains 32×50,000=1,600,000 pairs. It is generated with 25-step Euler sampling and α=1.5.

Guidance creates a tradeoff (Appendix Figure 16). At α=1.0, edges are blurry and details are distorted. At α≥2.0, oversaturation becomes increasingly visible. The paper uses 1.5.

Applications (Section 4, Appendix E)
  • Fast previews use one-step generation at 512×512, taking about 0.1 s. The result is interpolated to 1024 and refined with SDXL-Refiner (Figure 10). The same budget then allows many more seeds to be previewed before refinement.
  • The latent spaces align. For the same noise and prompt, the 0.9B / 1.7B one-step models produce nearly the same images as 25-step 2-RF (Figure 17). The one-step model can serve as a proxy for studying the continuous flow's latent space.
  • Encoding and reconstruction use 2-RF in both directions. The reverse ODE maps an image to noise. The forward ODE reconstructs the image:
X₀=X₁+∫₁⁰ v(Xₜ,t)dt（encode）
X̂₁=X₀+∫₀¹ v(Xₜ,t)dt（decode）
  • Both encoding and decoding use Euler sampling. Reconstruction succeeds with 4 steps. Because 2-RF is straighter, encoding can even use 1 step (Figure 26).
  • InstaFlow unexpectedly works directly with ControlNet models pretrained for SD. These include pose, depth, and edge conditioning (Figure 27). This suggests that the one-step model largely preserves SD's feature space.
Limitations (Section 5)

Complex compositional prompts remain a weakness. The authors suggest that longer training and more data could help. Appendix D notes that 2-RF has not fully converged. Distillation converges faster than reflow, and switching to LPIPS improves image quality immediately. Image quality still trails GigaGAN and multi-step SD.

Questions covered by the note

1. Failure of direct SD distillation. The curved PF-ODE gives an irregular noise ↔ image map. FID-5k remains ≥40 after the grid search.

  • 2. Reflow's contribution to distillation. The distribution stays unchanged, but the pairing becomes straighter and its convex cost decreases. The teacher-student FID change narrows from 22.8 → 40.9 to 22.1 → 31.0.
  • 3. The meaning of “2” in 2-RF. v₁=vSD, and one reflow operation produces v₂.
  • 4. The reason for using only one reflow operation. Repeated reflow is expensive and accumulates errors. The 3-RF results worsen for 25-step sampling and Stacked distillation. Its learning rate also has to decrease to 10⁻⁷.
  • 5. The source of the 199-day total. 16+11.2+64+54.4+53.6=199.2. The 1.7B model adds 39.6 days.
  • 6. The advantage over PD. At comparable distillation cost, one-step FID is 23.4 versus 37.2. The 1.7B model reaches 22.4 in 0.12 s. This improves on 2-step PD at 26.0 and 0.13 s.
  • 7. The choice of α. Larger α raises both CLIP and FID. α=1.5 provides the compromise.
  • 8. The need for real images. Training needs only the prompt dataset D𝒯.
  • 9. One-step 2-RF without distillation. Its FID-5k is 47.0 for SD 1.5 and 68.3 for SD 1.4. Two-step 2-RF gives 31.3. Distillation remains the necessary final step.
  • 10. The L2-to-LPIPS training sequence. Appendix D specifies this pipeline. The authors observe an immediate image-quality improvement after the switch to LPIPS. The L2 stage accounts for about half the distillation cost, at 54.4/108.
  • 11. The 1.7B model's 39.6-day cost. It starts directly from the 2-RF teacher already trained in the 0.9B pipeline and only adds distillation. The paper does not specify whether data-generation cost is counted separately.
  • 12. The difference from GAN training. There is no discriminator or minimax objective. Training regresses toward a fixed target and remains stable. One-step quality is nevertheless limited by the 2-RF teacher.
  • 13. The evidence in Figure 6. Reflow sharply reduces S(Z), and pixel trajectories approach straight lines. This is the condition that makes one-step Euler sampling useful.
